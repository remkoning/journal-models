# Changelog v3 (revision of v1)

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 3: val accuracy 88.3% (vs SMJ 100.0%, vs MgmtSci 76.7%); best so far 88.3%, threshold 87.3%. Revision of v1.

Train pool: v1 judged 39 train pairs, accuracy 96.2% (vs SMJ 97.4%, vs MgmtSci 95.0%). Reflection saw 2 misjudged, 7 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Theorizes how audiences such as investors, analysts, customers or job seekers read signals, claims, categories or actor attributes to form evaluations.
  - *now*: Theorizes how audiences read signals, claims, categories or actor attributes to form evaluations, emphasizing social meaning-making rather than testing economic demand or discrimination models.
  - *why*: The rule wrongly favored consumer-response tests of economic discrimination theory based on market revenues over within-organization network studies.
- **EDITED**: Links individual cognition or behavior to organizational systems, structures, governance arrangements or group processes, explaining how context changes what individuals do.
  - *now*: Links individual cognition or behavior to organizational structures, formal arrangements or group processes, e.g., how contract terms or hierarchy create pressures that shape decisions.
  - *why*: OS published executive-contract work explaining decisions through felt pressures; the board did not credit this behavioral link.
- **EDITED**: Uses quasi-experiments or field data to tell theoretical mechanisms apart (e.g., symbolic versus substantive), not only to test whether an intervention works.
  - *now*: Uses experiments, quasi-experiments or field data to separate mechanisms or reveal hidden trade-offs of a management practice, not only whether it works.
  - *why*: OS experiments on management practices that reveal trade-offs or interacting effects were underweighted against the alternatives.
- **EDITED**: Accepts formal, computational or simulation models that derive propositions about actors' beliefs, misalignment, learning or organizational processes.
  - *now*: Accepts formal or simulation models of organizational decision processes, such as judgment aggregation, learning, search or unintended consequences of practices, deriving propositions rather than optimal incentive designs.
  - *why*: OS formal models study decision aggregation or unintended consequences; principal-optimization models went to other outlets, and the old rule did not tell them apart.
- **ADDED**: Theorizes interplay between formal controls (monitoring, incentives, contracts) and informal or normative ones (values, trust, relationships), including substitution or crowding out.
  - *why*: OS articles combined agency and relational views or studied trust-based exchange; the board misread these as economics.
- **ADDED**: Explains individual or team outcomes through intraorganizational relationships, networks, social integration or position in formal and informal hierarchies.
  - *why*: OS picks centered on prior relationships, homophily and hierarchy position within organizations, while counterparts used human-capital or market logic.
- **EDITED**: Economics-style evaluation of a program, law or practice using exogenous variation, closing with efficiency or policy prescriptions instead of organizational theory.
  - *now*: Economics-style effect estimation, such as percentage-point gaps or policy evaluation using exogenous variation, closing with efficiency or policy prescriptions rather than organizational mechanism.
  - *why*: The non-OS articles reported percentage-point gaps; the old wording also penalized OS experiments on organizational practices.
- **EDITED**: Formal incentive or contracting models centered on leverage, wages or effort efficiency, backed by stylized correlations, suggest economics or finance journals.
  - *now*: Principal-agent, mechanism-design or relational-contract models where a principal optimizes incentives, matching or challenges; self-enforcement or discounted-value logic suggests economics journals.
  - *why*: Non-OS articles used principal-agent optimization, matching mechanisms and self-enforcing contract logic; the board needed a sharper cue.
- **EDITED**: Framed mainly around competitive advantage, industry evolution or strategic investment outcomes for strategy audiences, with thin sociological or behavioral mechanism.
  - *now*: Framed for strategy audiences, such as competitive advantage, M&A or capability performance, or industry evolution, with thin behavioral mechanism; also literature reviews or research agendas.
  - *why*: A counterpart framed around M&A capability microfoundations for corporate strategy readers, and a review anchor came from a strategy outlet.
- **EDITED**: The main explanation is cross-national culture, legal origins or country institutions, with legal or regulatory implications, which points to international business or governance outlets.
  - *now*: Main explanation is cross-national legal origins or country regulation with legal implications; broad social context like generalized trust is fine when theorized as shaping organizing.
  - *why*: An OS article explained firm sourcing with regional generalized trust, and the old flag nearly penalized it.
- **EDITED**: Explains outcomes through individual skill or human-capital advantages from learning-by-doing, without social, cognitive or organizational process.
  - *now*: Explains career or productivity outcomes through individual skill or human-capital accumulation, without social, relational, cognitive or organizational process.
  - *why*: A non-OS mobility study explained career gains through skill development, while the OS counterpart explained outcomes through relationships.
- **ADDED**: Tests a classic economic theory, such as taste-based discrimination, network effects or foreclosure, against market outcomes like prices, revenues or sales.
  - *why*: Several non-OS articles tested economic theories on market outcomes; these were sometimes confused with OS audience-evaluation work.
