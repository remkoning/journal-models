# Changelog v2 (revision of v1)

**REJECTED** at iteration 2: val accuracy 83.3% (vs SMJ 100.0%, vs MgmtSci 66.7%); best so far 88.3%, threshold 87.3%. Revision of v1.

Train pool: v1 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 81.2% (vs SMJ 90.0%, vs MgmtSci 72.5%). Reflection saw 8 misjudged, 1 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Theorizes how audiences such as investors, analysts, customers or job seekers read signals, claims, categories or actor attributes to form evaluations.
  - *now*: Theorizes how audiences read signals, claims, categories or actor attributes, and how those evaluations constrain or enable what organizations can do.
  - *why*: Organization Science audience papers trace audience perception back to what the firm can and cannot do, which separates them from audience-perception papers published elsewhere.
- **EDITED**: Explains with sociological or behavioral constructs such as status, legitimacy, categories, identity, authenticity, cognition, tokenism or symbolic compliance, not mainly economic efficiency.
  - *now*: Explains with sociological or behavioral constructs such as status, legitimacy, identity, authenticity, trust, reciprocity, reputation or symbolic compliance, not mainly economic efficiency.
  - *why*: Relational mechanisms (trust, reciprocity, reputation) marked an Organization Science nonmarket-strategy paper that the board missed.
- **EDITED**: Links individual cognition or behavior to organizational systems, structures, governance arrangements or group processes, explaining how context changes what individuals do.
  - *now*: Links individual cognition or perceptions to organizational structures, governance or group processes, explaining how individual reactions aggregate into collective decisions or outcomes.
  - *why*: Organization Science team papers theorize emergent processes from individual perceptions to collective choices; the board's confidence there was low.
- **EDITED**: Advances core organization-theory conversations: organizational learning, exploration and exploitation, search, optimal distinctiveness, categories, entrepreneurial creation, status dynamics.
  - *now*: Advances core organization-theory conversations: organizational learning, legitimacy, optimal distinctiveness, categories, status dynamics, entrepreneurial creation, inequality inside organizations.
  - *why*: The search and exploration cues pulled the board toward non-Organization Science platform and user-innovation papers; legitimacy and inequality inside organizations were missing.
- **EDITED**: Uses quasi-experiments or field data to tell theoretical mechanisms apart (e.g., symbolic versus substantive), not only to test whether an intervention works.
  - *now*: Uses quasi-experiments, experiments or field data to tell theoretical mechanisms apart (e.g., symbolic versus substantive), not only to test whether an intervention works.
  - *why*: Organization Science papers using experiments to distinguish mechanisms were under-credited.
- **ADDED**: Welcomes inductive qualitative studies that build new constructs showing how organizational practices produce identity tensions, inequality or unintended consequences.
  - *why*: An interview-based Organization Science paper on identity and unintended consequences lost to a large-sample quantitative paper.
- **ADDED**: Theorizes temporal or reciprocal dynamics, e.g., how status, evaluation and performance feed back, reinforce or decouple over repeated rounds.
  - *why*: An Organization Science paper on status and performance feeding back or decoupling over time was not credited.
- **ADDED**: Accepts organizational-economics arguments about interorganizational arrangements, firm boundaries or vertical structure when they explain organizational form and capabilities.
  - *why*: An Organization Science paper on collaborative arrangements and vertical structure lost to a measurement paper.
- **EDITED**: The main contribution is a new measure, dataset or descriptive typology rather than a theoretical mechanism about organizations or audiences.
  - *now*: The main contribution is a new measure, index, dataset or typology, even when pitched as enabling future theory, rather than a theoretical mechanism.
  - *why*: The board still favored a measure-centred paper because it claimed to support theory development.
- **EDITED**: Framed mainly around competitive advantage, industry evolution or strategic investment outcomes for strategy audiences, with thin sociological or behavioral mechanism.
  - *now*: Framed around competitive advantage, capability deployment, scale or scope advantages, or industry emergence for strategy audiences, with thin sociological or behavioral mechanism.
  - *why*: Capability-deployment internationalization papers are the strategy-journal signature; the broad 'industry evolution' wording wrongly penalized Organization Science structural papers.
- **EDITED**: The main explanation is cross-national culture, legal origins or country institutions, with legal or regulatory implications, which points to international business or governance outlets.
  - *now*: Explanation hinges on cross-national culture, legal origins, or one country's distinctive regime or campaign, rather than a general organizational mechanism such as legitimacy.
  - *why*: The flag hit an Organization Science paper set in developing economies whose mechanism was legitimacy, and missed the single-country campaign framing of a non-Organization Science paper.
- **EDITED**: Openly exploratory pattern-finding whose mechanisms are guessed after the fact, with no theoretical argument set out in advance.
  - *now*: Quantitative pattern-finding whose mechanisms are guessed after the fact; this does not apply to inductive qualitative theory-building.
  - *why*: The flag wrongly penalized an inductive interview study that Organization Science published.
- **ADDED**: Applies decision biases or effect estimates to online platform or contest data, headlining precise percentages or design prescriptions rather than social mechanisms.
  - *why*: Non-Organization Science papers on platform data led with loss-aversion effects, percentage gaps or advice on choosing a search mode.
