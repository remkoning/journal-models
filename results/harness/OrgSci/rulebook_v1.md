# Organization Science rulebook v1

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 1: val accuracy 88.3% (vs SMJ 100.0%, vs MgmtSci 76.7%); best so far 80.0%, threshold 79.0%. Revision of v0.

## What Organization Science values
1. Theorizes how audiences such as investors, analysts, customers or job seekers read signals, claims, categories or actor attributes to form evaluations.
2. Posits countervailing effects, paradoxes or trade-offs, such as a benefit at one stage or condition and a penalty at another, and specifies contingencies.
3. Explains with sociological or behavioral constructs such as status, legitimacy, categories, identity, authenticity, cognition, tokenism or symbolic compliance, not mainly economic efficiency.
4. Links individual cognition or behavior to organizational systems, structures, governance arrangements or group processes, explaining how context changes what individuals do.
5. Advances core organization-theory conversations: organizational learning, exploration and exploitation, search, optimal distinctiveness, categories, entrepreneurial creation, status dynamics.
6. Frames the contribution as resolving a tension between prevailing theory and emerging evidence, or between competing mechanisms, not just estimating an effect.
7. Uses quasi-experiments or field data to tell theoretical mechanisms apart (e.g., symbolic versus substantive), not only to test whether an intervention works.
8. Accepts formal, computational or simulation models that derive propositions about actors' beliefs, misalignment, learning or organizational processes.
9. Builds moderators from named theoretical mechanisms about actors, audiences or organizations, rather than from convenient proxies or practical boundary conditions.

## What Organization Science does not value / red flags
1. Economics-style evaluation of a program, law or practice using exogenous variation, closing with efficiency or policy prescriptions instead of organizational theory.
2. Formal incentive or contracting models centered on leverage, wages or effort efficiency, backed by stylized correlations, suggest economics or finance journals.
3. The main contribution is a new measure, dataset or descriptive typology rather than a theoretical mechanism about organizations or audiences.
4. Framed mainly around competitive advantage, industry evolution or strategic investment outcomes for strategy audiences, with thin sociological or behavioral mechanism.
5. The main explanation is cross-national culture, legal origins or country institutions, with legal or regulatory implications, which points to international business or governance outlets.
6. Openly exploratory pattern-finding whose mechanisms are guessed after the fact, with no theoretical argument set out in advance.
7. Explains outcomes through individual skill or human-capital advantages from learning-by-doing, without social, cognitive or organizational process.
