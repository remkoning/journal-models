# Changelog v4 (revision of v3)

**REJECTED** at iteration 4: val accuracy 86.7% (vs SMJ 100.0%, vs MgmtSci 73.3%); best so far 88.3%, threshold 87.3%. Revision of v3.

Train pool: v3 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 81.2% (vs SMJ 92.5%, vs MgmtSci 70.0%). Reflection saw 8 misjudged, 1 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Links individual cognition or behavior to organizational structures, formal arrangements or group processes, e.g., how contract terms or hierarchy create pressures that shape decisions.
  - *now*: Links levels: how organizational status, structure, hierarchy, life stage or field-level actors shape or condition individual, team or organizational behavior and outcomes.
  - *why*: Several Organization Science winners were distinguished by cross-level theorizing: organization status moderating individual pay, population-level actors shaping firm learning, and subgroups across hierarchical levels.
- **EDITED**: Advances core organization-theory conversations: organizational learning, exploration and exploitation, search, optimal distinctiveness, categories, entrepreneurial creation, status dynamics.
  - *now*: Advances core organization-theory conversations: organizational learning, behavioral theory of the firm, attention, exploration and exploitation, search, categories, entrepreneurial creation, status dynamics.
  - *why*: Behavioral-theory and attention-based framing marked an Organization Science article that lost out to a methods paper on a similar topic.
- **EDITED**: Uses experiments, quasi-experiments or field data to separate mechanisms or reveal hidden trade-offs of a management practice, not only whether it works.
  - *now*: Uses experiments or multimethod designs to isolate actor or audience mechanisms theorized in advance, rather than mainly reporting effect sizes of a practice's consequences.
  - *why*: The old wording rewarded empirical papers on a practice's unintended consequences that report percentage effects, which led the board to favor non-Organization Science articles.
- **EDITED**: Accepts formal or simulation models of organizational decision processes, such as judgment aggregation, learning, search or unintended consequences of practices, deriving propositions rather than optimal incentive designs.
  - *now*: Accepts formal or simulation models of organizational processes, such as learning, search, judgment aggregation, or divergent beliefs among organizational actors, deriving testable propositions.
  - *why*: Organization Science published a formal model built around belief misalignment among governance actors, even though it included incentive elements; the old rule penalized it.
- **EDITED**: Explains individual or team outcomes through intraorganizational relationships, networks, social integration or position in formal and informal hierarchies.
  - *now*: Explains individual or team outcomes through relationships, networks, social integration or hierarchy position, including how actors' beliefs about hierarchy shape tie formation.
  - *why*: The Organization Science side theorized how beliefs about status shape networking, while the other side focused on status and individual effort.
- **ADDED**: Contextualizes familiar individual-level effects (pay gaps, human capital, status) by showing which organizational conditions amplify, erase or reverse them.
  - *why*: The Organization Science winners took known individual effects and made them depend on organizational context; the board underrated this.
- **EDITED**: Economics-style effect estimation, such as percentage-point gaps or policy evaluation using exogenous variation, closing with efficiency or policy prescriptions rather than organizational mechanism.
  - *now*: Economics-style effect estimation or tests of classic economic theories (demand-side discrimination, real options, network effects) against wages, prices or sales, closing with efficiency or policy prescriptions.
  - *why*: Merged the old classic-economic-theory flag into this one, adding demand-side wage tests and real-option decision models, both seen on non-Organization Science sides.
- **DROPPED**: Tests a classic economic theory, such as taste-based discrimination, network effects or foreclosure, against market outcomes like prices, revenues or sales.
  - *why*: Merged into the economics-style estimation flag to free a slot.
- **EDITED**: Principal-agent, mechanism-design or relational-contract models where a principal optimizes incentives, matching or challenges; self-enforcement or discounted-value logic suggests economics journals.
  - *now*: Principal-agent or mechanism-design models where a principal optimizes incentives with no role for divergent beliefs, cognition or organizational process.
  - *why*: A formal agency model centered on belief misalignment appeared in Organization Science; the flag should hit only pure incentive optimization.
- **EDITED**: The main contribution is a new measure, dataset or descriptive typology rather than a theoretical mechanism about organizations or audiences.
  - *now*: The main contribution is a new measure, dataset, research method, computational technique or descriptive typology rather than a theoretical mechanism about organizations or audiences.
  - *why*: A paper introducing a new computational method for researchers was favored over a theory-testing Organization Science article.
- **EDITED**: Framed for strategy audiences, such as competitive advantage, M&A or capability performance, or industry evolution, with thin behavioral mechanism; also literature reviews or research agendas.
  - *now*: Corporate-strategy outcomes (divestiture, diversification, M&A, competitive advantage) tested on firm panels with audience or social mechanisms only inferred; also literature reviews or research agendas.
  - *why*: Identity or status language on corporate-portfolio panels fooled the board. Meanwhile, a competitive-advantage opening with relational theory was wrongly penalized.
- **EDITED**: Openly exploratory pattern-finding whose mechanisms are guessed after the fact, with no theoretical argument set out in advance.
  - *now*: Exploratory or null-result pattern-finding whose mechanism is guessed afterward, e.g., results described only as 'suggestive of' a mechanism, with no prior theoretical argument.
  - *why*: A non-Organization Science paper that offered an organizational-learning mechanism only as a post hoc interpretation of negative results was not flagged.
- **EDITED**: Explains career or productivity outcomes through individual skill or human-capital accumulation, without social, relational, cognitive or organizational process.
  - *now*: Explains outcomes through individual skill or human-capital accumulation alone; human capital is fine when conditioned by organizational structure, power or life stage.
  - *why*: This flag wrongly penalized an Organization Science article that made founding-team human capital depend on decision structure and life stage.
- **ADDED**: Framed mainly as advancing individual psychology (effort, motivation, psychological experience) without consequences for relationships, networks, social structure or organizing.
  - *why*: The non-Organization Science side of a status pair framed its contribution as understanding the psychological experience of status and effort.
