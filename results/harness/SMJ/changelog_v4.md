# Changelog v4 (revision of v1)

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 4: val accuracy 93.3% (vs OrgSci 93.3%, vs MgmtSci 93.3%); best so far 93.3%, threshold 92.3%. Revision of v1.

Train pool: v1 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 92.5% (vs OrgSci 95.0%, vs MgmtSci 90.0%). Reflection saw 3 misjudged, 6 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Individual or employee-level mechanisms (micro-foundations) are explicitly tied to firm-level results, such as leaders affecting performance or worker interventions raising revenue.
  - *now*: Individual-level mechanisms count when they explain strategic processes (search, competitive response, decision-making) or firm results; lab or manager experiments qualify.
  - *why*: Lab experiments on organizational search and manager experiments on competitive response were SMJ, while an employee intervention study on turnover was not.
- **EDITED**: Hypotheses are derived from core strategy theories (resource-based, real options, behavioral theory of the firm, capabilities, competitive dynamics) and tested on archival or field-experimental data.
  - *now*: Hypotheses come from core strategy theories (resource-based, real options, behavioral theory, capabilities, competitive dynamics, transaction costs) and are tested with archival, experimental or qualitative evidence.
  - *why*: SMJ articles used lab experiments and qualitative evidence, so a rule limited to archival or field data misled the board.
- **ADDED**: Reviews, essays or conceptual pieces that synthesize, extend or guide use of a strategy theory for strategy researchers fit, even without new data.
  - *why*: SMJ published reviews and conceptual extensions of strategy theories, while other journals took the formal-model and empirical counterparts.
- **ADDED**: Network positions, prior relationships or stakeholder ties are treated as firm-level sources of advantage, risk or boundary choices, often under environmental shocks.
  - *why*: SMJ papers framed brokerage, relational capital and stakeholder risk as firm advantage or risk; the board underweighted this framing.
- **ADDED**: Qualitative studies fit when they examine how strategy-makers formulate, justify or communicate strategy and explicitly link findings to strategy research.
  - *why*: A qualitative study of strategy-makers' discursive practices was SMJ, which goes against a blanket penalty on qualitative work.
- **EDITED**: A formal or analytical model deriving optimal design, pricing or fee policies without empirical testing suggests an operations, economics or marketing journal.
  - *now*: The main contribution is a formal model deriving optimal policies, equilibria or new mathematical constructs, without empirical testing or a synthesis for strategy research.
  - *why*: Formal papers that build new constructs went elsewhere, while SMJ took reviews of the same formal approach; the rule needs to separate the two.
- **EDITED**: An economics or finance framing: testing information asymmetry with market-price predictions, macro or local economic outcomes, or impacts at the level of public policy.
  - *now*: Economics or finance framing: corporate-finance mechanisms, market-price tests, IO-style effect-size estimates without management theory, or macro and public-policy outcomes.
  - *why*: An IO-style entry-deterrence test and an internal capital markets paper went elsewhere, while their theory-driven strategy counterparts were SMJ.
- **EDITED**: Personnel or labor outcomes such as exit, promotion, aspirations or gender gaps, studied without any link to firm strategy or firm performance.
  - *now*: The dependent variable is a personnel outcome such as turnover, exit, promotion, aspirations or gender gaps, even when it is framed as relevant to the firm.
  - *why*: A turnover study framed as relevant to the firm still went elsewhere, so framing it that way does not rescue an HR outcome.
- **EDITED**: A sociological focus on categories, identity or evaluator perception where the producer is an individual and the firm's strategic value is absent.
  - *now*: A sociological focus on categories, identity or evaluator perception, framed mainly within organization theory, where the firm's strategic value is absent.
  - *why*: A diversification-authenticity study positioned in organization theory went elsewhere even though the producer was a firm.
- **EDITED**: An inductive comparative case study that builds a process theory from a handful of firms suggests a qualitative organization-theory outlet.
  - *now*: An inductive case study that builds process theory framed in organization theory, with no tie to strategy-making or strategy literatures, suggests another outlet.
  - *why*: SMJ published inductive work on strategy-makers, so the flag should apply only when the strategy link is missing.
- **EDITED**: Purely conceptual or simulation arguments about organizational adaptation, or about reconciling theoretical paradigms, with no empirical test of strategic outcomes.
  - *now*: Conceptual or simulation arguments about organizational adaptation or reconciling paradigms that are not anchored in a core strategy theory.
  - *why*: A conceptual extension of the resource-based view was SMJ, so being conceptual is not a red flag in itself.
- **ADDED**: The abstract mainly describes an intervention or training design and its causal effect, without theory-derived hypotheses about strategic choices or firm outcomes.
  - *why*: Abstracts centred on an RCT's design or intervention went elsewhere, while hypothesis-driven strategy counterparts on the same topic were SMJ.
