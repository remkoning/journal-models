## What Strategic Management Journal values
1. The firm, or the founder acting for the venture, is the decision-maker, and the abstract studies a strategic choice such as entry, positioning, investment, scope or innovation direction.
2. The outcomes are at firm level: performance, value creation, growth-option value, revenue or competitive advantage, not only individual or audience outcomes.
3. Effects depend on contingencies: the abstract shows how strategy–environment fit or firm and industry characteristics strengthen or weaken the main relationship.
4. The abstract names the costs, trade-offs or unintended penalties of strategic moves for the firm, such as change, repositioning, path dependence or departing from tradition.
5. Micro-level mechanisms are tied to firm-level drivers or results in either direction: firm growth or strategy shaping employees, or employees and departures shaping firm performance.
6. Strategic leadership and governance: CEO traits or biases measured archivally, top teams, board composition, family control or succession, linked to firm strategy or performance.
7. Hypotheses are derived from core strategy theories (resource-based, Penrosean growth, real options, behavioral theory of the firm, capabilities, absorptive capacity, competitive dynamics) applied to firms.
8. The abstract explicitly claims contributions to strategy literatures, such as competitive, corporate, nonmarket, complementor, human-capital or entrepreneurial strategy, and states what managers should do.
9. Customer or audience reactions are framed as determinants of the value or cost of a firm's strategic choice, not as phenomena of evaluator cognition in their own right.
10. Cognition or emotion of top or founding teams is studied as a driver of strategic decisions such as persisting, escalating commitment, or exiting a venture.
11. Exogenous shocks or natural experiments identify how firm resources, ties, human capital or technology adoption causally change firm innovation, scope or performance.

## What Strategic Management Journal does not value / red flags
1. A formal or analytical model deriving optimal design, pricing or fee policies without empirical testing suggests an operations, economics or marketing journal.
2. Economics framing: market-price tests of information asymmetry, macro or policy outcomes, or partner-firm experiments evaluating an HR practice's productivity without strategy theory.
3. Personnel or labor outcomes such as exit, promotion, aspirations, motivation or gender gaps, studied without any link to firm strategy or firm performance.
4. A sociological focus on categories, identity, collective action or intra-team exchange and free riding, where the firm's strategic choice and value are absent.
5. Inductive case or mixed-methods theory-building (interviews, field observation, iterative build-and-test cycles) suggests a qualitative organization-theory outlet.
6. Purely conceptual or simulation arguments about organizational adaptation, or about reconciling theoretical paradigms, with no empirical test of strategic outcomes.
7. The main contribution is settling a debate between theories of the firm, rather than explaining a strategic choice and what follows from it.
8. International-business framing centered on foreign versus domestic firms, MNC host-country networks or national institutional features suggests an international business outlet.
