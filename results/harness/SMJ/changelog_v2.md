# Changelog v2 (revision of v1)

**REJECTED** at iteration 2: val accuracy 88.3% (vs OrgSci 83.3%, vs MgmtSci 93.3%); best so far 93.3%, threshold 92.3%. Revision of v1.

Train pool: v1 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 95.0% (vs OrgSci 97.5%, vs MgmtSci 92.5%). Reflection saw 3 misjudged, 6 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Individual or employee-level mechanisms (micro-foundations) are explicitly tied to firm-level results, such as leaders affecting performance or worker interventions raising revenue.
  - *now*: Micro-level mechanisms are tied to firm-level drivers or results in either direction: firm growth or strategy shaping employees, or employees and departures shaping firm performance.
  - *why*: SMJ also publishes employee outcomes explained by firm-level drivers such as growth, not only micro-to-firm effects.
- **EDITED**: The study covers strategic leadership and governance: top teams, dominant coalitions, family control or CEO succession, and their link to firm strategy or performance feedback.
  - *now*: Strategic leadership and governance: CEO traits or biases measured archivally, top teams, board composition, family control or succession, linked to firm strategy or performance.
  - *why*: Executive-bias and board-composition studies of large public firms were underweighted compared with entrepreneur-level studies.
- **EDITED**: Hypotheses are derived from core strategy theories (resource-based, real options, behavioral theory of the firm, capabilities, competitive dynamics) and tested on archival or field-experimental data.
  - *now*: Hypotheses are derived from core strategy theories (resource-based, Penrosean growth, real options, behavioral theory of the firm, capabilities, absorptive capacity, competitive dynamics) applied to firms.
  - *why*: Grounding in strategy theory applied to firms separated SMJ abstracts; when real options was applied to individual investors, it misled the board.
- **EDITED**: The abstract explicitly claims contributions to strategy literatures, such as competitive, corporate, complementor, entrepreneurial or international strategy, and states what managers should do.
  - *now*: The abstract explicitly claims contributions to strategy literatures, such as competitive, corporate, nonmarket, complementor, human-capital or entrepreneurial strategy, and states what managers should do.
  - *why*: Abstracts framed around MNCs or foreign versus domestic firms went to IB outlets, while nonmarket and human-capital framings went to SMJ.
- **ADDED**: Cognition or emotion of top or founding teams is studied as a driver of strategic decisions such as persisting, escalating commitment, or exiting a venture.
  - *why*: SMJ published team-psychology work when it explained decisions to persist with or exit a venture.
- **ADDED**: Exogenous shocks or natural experiments identify how firm resources, ties, human capital or technology adoption causally change firm innovation, scope or performance.
  - *why*: Several SMJ abstracts used shock-based identification to show how firm resources or ties affect innovation or performance.
- **EDITED**: An economics or finance framing: testing information asymmetry with market-price predictions, macro or local economic outcomes, or impacts at the level of public policy.
  - *now*: Economics framing: market-price tests of information asymmetry, macro or policy outcomes, or partner-firm experiments evaluating an HR practice's productivity without strategy theory.
  - *why*: A personnel-economics field experiment that evaluated a management practice went elsewhere, while a theory-grounded SMJ study was published.
- **EDITED**: Personnel or labor outcomes such as exit, promotion, aspirations or gender gaps, studied without any link to firm strategy or firm performance.
  - *now*: Personnel or labor outcomes such as exit, promotion, aspirations, motivation or gender gaps, studied without any link to firm strategy or firm performance.
  - *why*: An employee-motivation experiment with no firm-level outcome lost to a study linking human capital to firm performance.
- **EDITED**: A sociological focus on categories, identity or evaluator perception where the producer is an individual and the firm's strategic value is absent.
  - *now*: A sociological focus on categories, identity, collective action or intra-team exchange and free riding, where the firm's strategic choice and value are absent.
  - *why*: A collective-action framing of resource contributions within teams marked the non-SMJ entrepreneurial-team abstract.
- **EDITED**: An inductive comparative case study that builds a process theory from a handful of firms suggests a qualitative organization-theory outlet.
  - *now*: Inductive case or mixed-methods theory-building (interviews, field observation, iterative build-and-test cycles) suggests a qualitative organization-theory outlet.
  - *why*: A mixed-methods theory-building study of knowledge transfer went elsewhere, while the purely experimental one was SMJ.
- **ADDED**: International-business framing centered on foreign versus domestic firms, MNC host-country networks or national institutional features suggests an international business outlet.
  - *why*: Abstracts on MNC knowledge networks and on domestic versus foreign firms under different institutions were the non-SMJ choices.
- **ADDED**: Investor screening of individual entrepreneurs' pitches or funding requests, modeled formally and tested experimentally, suggests an entrepreneurial-finance or management-science outlet.
  - *why*: A formal model of investors evaluating pitches, tested in a field experiment, lost to an archival study of executive bias.
