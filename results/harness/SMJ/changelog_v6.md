# Changelog v6 (revision of v5)

**REJECTED** at iteration 6: val accuracy 90.0% (vs OrgSci 93.3%, vs MgmtSci 86.7%); best so far 93.3%, threshold 92.3%. Revision of v5.

Train pool: v5 judged 39 train pairs, accuracy 89.7% (vs OrgSci 89.5%, vs MgmtSci 90.0%). Reflection saw 4 misjudged, 5 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: The outcomes are at firm level: performance, value creation, growth-option value, revenue or competitive advantage, not only individual or audience outcomes.
  - *now*: Outcomes are firm-level (performance, value, innovation, growth, competitive advantage), or employee outcomes explained by firm-level strategic conditions such as growth, scaling or scope.
  - *why*: Employee outcomes explained by firm growth dynamics through strategy theory were published in SMJ, so the old rule was too strict.
- **EDITED**: Individual-level mechanisms count when they explain strategic actions or firm results; lab, manager or in-firm field experiments that measure firm-level outcomes such as revenue qualify.
  - *now*: Lab or field experiments fit when they manipulate a managerial or organizational lever (monitoring, hierarchy, incentives, authority) and link it to strategic search or decisions.
  - *why*: SMJ's experiments manipulate managerial levers. Experiments on individual traits, or management-practice experiments framed in economics terms, went to other journals, even when they reported revenue.
- **EDITED**: Governance and leadership: boards, top teams, CEO succession or dismissal, and executive motives such as status rivalry that drive strategic actions and their value consequences.
  - *now*: Governance and leadership: boards, top teams, CEO succession, and executive traits or biases (overconfidence, founder status, status rivalry) shaping strategic actions, disclosures or firm value.
  - *why*: An upper-echelons study of executive bias in large firms was published in SMJ, but the board missed it because the rule only covered motives.
- **EDITED**: Hypotheses come from core strategy theories (resource-based, real options, behavioral theory, capabilities, competitive dynamics, transaction costs) and are tested with archival, experimental or qualitative evidence.
  - *now*: Hypotheses come from core strategy theories: resource-based, Penrosean growth, real options, behavioral theory, capabilities, competitive dynamics, transaction costs, strategic human capital.
  - *why*: Penrosean growth theory and strategic human capital theory marked SMJ articles but were not on the list; the clause on evidence types did not help tell journals apart.
- **EDITED**: Network positions, political connections or stakeholder ties are treated as firm-level sources of advantage, resource access, risk or boundary choices, often under environmental shocks.
  - *now*: Political connections, corporate political activity or stakeholder ties are treated as firm-level assets or liabilities affecting value, innovation or risk, often identified through shocks.
  - *why*: SMJ political-strategy papers treat ties as liabilities as well as assets and use firm value or innovation as outcomes. The board underweighted these papers.
- **EDITED**: The main contribution is a formal model deriving optimal policies, equilibria or new mathematical constructs, without empirical testing or a synthesis for strategy research.
  - *now*: A formal model deriving optimal policies or equilibria for markets, operations or public policy; models of firm strategy problems with managerial implications are acceptable.
  - *why*: SMJ published a game-theoretic model of a multinational's CSR strategy, so a blanket penalty on formal models misleads the board.
- **EDITED**: Economics or finance framing: corporate-finance mechanisms, market-price tests, IO-style effect-size estimates without management theory, or macro and public-policy outcomes.
  - *now*: Economics framing: macro or public-policy outcomes, or IO-style effect estimates without management theory; investor or market reactions are fine when tied to firm strategy or leadership.
  - *why*: SMJ articles used abnormal returns and investor perception moderated by firm political strategy or CEO type. The market-price penalty caused misjudgments.
- **EDITED**: The outcome is a personnel or personal reward (pay, turnover, promotion, executive compensation, gender gaps) with no measured effect on firm performance or strategic action.
  - *now*: Personnel outcomes (pay, promotion, turnover, gender gaps, motivation) studied without strategy theory linking them to firm growth, strategic action or performance.
  - *why*: Employee outcomes are acceptable when strategy theory links them to firm strategy. The flag should target studies that lack that link.
- **EDITED**: A sociological focus on categories, identity or evaluator perception, framed mainly within organization theory, where the firm's strategic value is absent.
  - *now*: A sociological focus on categories, identity, legitimacy or decoupling, framed within organization or institutional theory, where the firm's strategic value is absent.
  - *why*: Studies framed around institutional decoupling and legitimacy appeared in the other journal, while firm-strategy treatments of similar topics went to SMJ.
- **EDITED**: An intervention or program is evaluated only on individual-level outcomes, without theory about strategic choices or firm-level results such as revenue or productivity.
  - *now*: Individual traits, preferences or training (cognitive styles, abilities, pitch skills) predicting individual behavior or outcomes, without a managerial lever or firm-level strategic outcome.
  - *why*: Papers on individual differences and individual training went elsewhere, even when they were framed as microfoundations or used a real-options model.
