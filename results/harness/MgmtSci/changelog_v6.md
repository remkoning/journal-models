# Changelog v6 (revision of v4)

**REJECTED** at iteration 6: val accuracy 93.3% (vs OrgSci 86.7%, vs SMJ 100.0%); best so far 96.7%, threshold 95.7%. Revision of v4.

Train pool: v4 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 100.0% (vs OrgSci 100.0%, vs SMJ 100.0%). Reflection saw 0 misjudged, 9 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Credible causal identification (policy shocks, sudden events, instruments, randomization) beats panel associations with moderators, even on large samples.
  - *now*: Credible causal identification (random or quasi-random assignment, unexpected deaths or shocks, instruments, policy changes) decisively beats panel associations with moderators, even with richer theory.
  - *why*: In several low-confidence pairs the identified study won over moderator-heavy panels; the rule needed more decisive weight.
- **EDITED**: A formal model (real options, matching, game, optimal choice) that derives predictions, especially when tested by field experiment or data, even if magnitudes are unstated.
  - *now*: A formal model (real options, matching, game, optimal choice) deriving predictions tested on field or archival data, ideally several datasets, even if magnitudes are unstated.
  - *why*: A model tested on multiple archival datasets beat a hand-collected replication study, but the board was unsure.
- **EDITED**: When both abstracts are sociological, prefer the one testing behavioral outcomes on large archival data over qualitative or multimethod case work.
  - *now*: On sociological or audience-perception topics, prefer large-scale behavioral evidence from archival or platform data over experiments, qualitative, or multimethod case work.
  - *why*: Platform-data audience study beat an experiment built around a coined paradox; the rule now covers experiments too.
- **EDITED**: Tests a common management practice (rankings, pay, hiring tools) on firm records, ruling out alternative mechanisms or showing standard remedies fail.
  - *now*: Traces an observed gap to a specific channel by explicitly ruling out competing explanations (selection, signaling, enforcer bias, anticipation).
  - *why*: Management Science winners decomposed effects and excluded rival channels; generalized beyond management practices.
- **EDITED**: Contribution framed around named literatures or coined labels (a new 'paradox', a named hypothesis, optimal distinctiveness, categories) rather than a substantive answer.
  - *now*: Contribution is a named literature or coined label (a 'paradox', named hypothesis, optimal distinctiveness) itself, not an identified, tested effect.
  - *why*: A Management Science winner introduced a new construct but backed it with an instrument; the label alone should not penalize it.
- **EDITED**: Long verbal theorizing ('we/I theorize', inverted U, lists of moderators) where the data merely confirm predicted directions without identification.
  - *now*: Long verbal theorizing ('we/I theorize', inverted U, moderation by life stage or structure, bridging perspectives) where data merely confirm directions without identification.
  - *why*: Losers framed contributions as contingent moderation or as bridging scholarly perspectives without identification.
- **EDITED**: Multimethod designs that combine a case narrative with hypothetical-vignette experiments to illustrate a perception mechanism.
  - *now*: Lab, online, or vignette experiments on stylized tasks or hypothetical perceptions, when the rival uses field randomization or real behavioral data.
  - *why*: Lab and real-effort experiments lost to field experiments and archival mechanism studies in multiple pairs.
