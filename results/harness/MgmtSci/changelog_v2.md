# Changelog v2 (revision of v0)

**REJECTED** at iteration 2: val accuracy 91.7% (vs OrgSci 90.0%, vs SMJ 93.3%); best so far 96.7%, threshold 95.7%. Revision of v0.

Train pool: v0 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 92.5% (vs OrgSci 85.0%, vs SMJ 100.0%). Reflection saw 3 misjudged, 6 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **ADDED**: Formal analytical models (game-theoretic, equilibrium, optimization) yielding conditions under which a decision or design choice is optimal are strongly favored.
  - *why*: Analytical models won every pair against empirical or conceptual strategy papers, including the anchors.
- **ADDED**: Models framed in economic terms (competition, incentives, welfare, platform or contract design) beat models framed around sociological concepts like legitimacy or stratification.
  - *why*: In a pair where both papers used formal models, the IO/welfare model won over the sociologically framed one.
- **ADDED**: Explicit causal identification: randomized or quasi-random assignment, natural experiments, unexpected shocks such as deaths, instruments, or fixed-effects designs isolating exogenous variation.
  - *why*: Several misjudged MS papers relied on randomization, an IV or exogenous deaths, while their counterparts were associational.
- **ADDED**: Tests a clearly specified economic or behavioral mechanism and explicitly rules out rival explanations, e.g., signaling, selection, or anticipatory departure.
  - *why*: The MS papers traced effects to a specific mechanism and excluded alternatives.
- **ADDED**: Reports economically interpretable magnitudes, such as percentile-equivalent or percentage effects, rather than only significance and direction of association.
  - *why*: The winning papers stated effect sizes that are meaningful in economic terms.
- **ADDED**: Uses observed behavior in real markets or organizations (sales, revenues, ratings, performance, transactions) at scale rather than hypothetical choices.
  - *why*: MS papers using box-office, platform-rating and inspection data beat papers built on experiments or network-survey data.
- **ADDED**: Directly tests a well-known economic theory or a common practitioner claim against data, even with a surprising or null result.
  - *why*: A test of customer-discrimination theory against a common employer claim beat a contingency-based network study.
- **ADDED**: Draws implications for how organizations, platforms, or evaluation processes should be designed, or for consumer welfare and policy.
  - *why*: The MS papers stressed design elements and welfare consequences.
- **ADDED**: Combines a theoretical model with several datasets, or separates individual-level from team-level effects using appropriate estimation.
  - *why*: Methodological rigor of this kind marked MS papers on entrepreneurial teams and employee mobility.
- **ADDED**: Contribution framed as bridging literatures, combining theoretical perspectives, or 'extending research on X' rather than answering a sharp question.
  - *why*: Several losing papers framed themselves as a 'meeting ground' for literatures or as combining perspectives.
- **ADDED**: Hypothesis-driven contingency stories stacking moderators (life stage, hierarchy level, orientation, tenure) on associational archival correlations without identification.
  - *why*: The losing human-capital, homophily and foreign-hire papers relied on moderator contingencies without causal designs.
- **ADDED**: Purely conceptual pieces, literature reviews, or commentary on modeling approaches without new formal results or evidence.
  - *why*: An anchor pair contrasts a new model with a review of modeling approaches.
- **ADDED**: Vignette or online experiments testing a theorized organizational paradox or perspective, where field data on market behavior would be expected instead.
  - *why*: Experiments testing the authenticity paradox and a values-versus-monitoring account of misconduct lost to field-data papers.
- **ADDED**: Institution-centric or organization-theory constructs (institutional factors, exploration, authenticity) as the main contribution, with mechanisms asserted rather than identified.
  - *why*: Losing papers centered on institutional factors or exploration and theorized mechanisms without isolating them.
- **ADDED**: Strategy-journal framing aimed at firms' competitive positioning ('informs complementor strategy') rather than mechanism, design, or welfare.
  - *why*: Anchor pairs show papers framed around strategy positioning appearing elsewhere.
