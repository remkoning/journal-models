# Changelog v5 (revision of v4)

**REJECTED** at iteration 5: val accuracy 90.0% (vs OrgSci 86.7%, vs SMJ 93.3%); best so far 96.7%, threshold 95.7%. Revision of v4.

Train pool: v4 judged 39 train pairs, accuracy 96.2% (vs OrgSci 92.1%, vs SMJ 100.0%). Reflection saw 2 misjudged, 7 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Economics-style mechanisms such as incentives, information asymmetry, signaling, matching, equilibrium, or explicit trade-offs, rather than legitimacy, identity, or institutional logics.
  - *now*: Economics-style mechanisms (incentives, information asymmetry, self-enforcing contracts, signaling, matching, equilibrium, trade-offs) rather than legitimacy, identity, culture, or institutional logics.
  - *why*: Relational-contract theory won over a generalized-trust/culture study despite the latter's huge sample.
- **EDITED**: Credible causal identification (policy shocks, sudden events, instruments, randomization) beats panel associations with moderators, even on large samples.
  - *now*: Credible causal identification (policy shocks, historical or sudden events, instruments, randomization) beats panel associations with moderators, even on large samples.
  - *why*: A historical natural event combined with a model won over hypothesis testing on industry panels.
- **EDITED**: A formal model (real options, matching, game, optimal choice) that derives predictions, especially when tested by field experiment or data, even if magnitudes are unstated.
  - *now*: Analytical formal model (principal-agent, game, optimal policy) with characterized solutions or sharp predictions; valued even without data or stated magnitudes.
  - *why*: A stylized principal-agent model with no data beat a field experiment only by a slim margin; the board needs more confidence here.
- **ADDED**: Computational or structural models count when fitted to experimental or field behavior to separate competing mechanisms, not as stand-alone simulations.
  - *why*: Experiment plus fitted computational model won, while a stand-alone simulation lost to an RCT.
- **EDITED**: A new, validated, reusable measure that predicts real outcomes; not a critique or correction of an existing measure.
  - *now*: A new, validated, reusable measure (e.g., text-based, experimentally checked) that predicts real outcomes; not a critique or correction of an existing measure.
  - *why*: A validated text-based measure tested on field data won but was judged with low confidence.
- **EDITED**: Large-sample archival, administrative, platform, or field data on actual behavior over small samples, vignettes, or single cases.
  - *now*: Large-sample archival, administrative, platform, or field data on actual behavior, but sharp theory-derived predictions outrank sheer sample size or broad regional proxies.
  - *why*: The board over-weighted million-firm samples against a theory-sharp test on a smaller sample.
- **ADDED**: Representative, population-scale measurement of an economically important phenomenon with precise, benchmarked magnitudes counts even without a causal design.
  - *why*: A nationally representative descriptive adoption study won over a coined-concept experiment; the board was at 50%.
- **EDITED**: Shows how decision makers (firms, employers, evaluators, boards) actually respond to constraints, with concrete managerial or policy implications.
  - *now*: Shows how decision makers respond to constraints, or tests a common management practice on real firms, with concrete managerial or policy implications.
  - *why*: Merged with the overlapping management-practice rule to free a slot.
- **DROPPED**: Tests a common management practice (rankings, pay, hiring tools) on firm records, ruling out alternative mechanisms or showing standard remedies fail.
  - *why*: Merged into the decision-maker response rule.
- **DROPPED**: When both abstracts are sociological, prefer the one testing behavioral outcomes on large archival data over qualitative or multimethod case work.
  - *why*: Redundant with the large-sample data rule and the qualitative-work red flag.
- **EDITED**: Questions about bias, discrimination, or inequality framed as testable demand-side or incentive mechanisms rather than as contextualizing institutional forces.
  - *now*: Bias, discrimination, inequality, or welfare questions framed as testable demand-side or incentive mechanisms, including counterintuitive efficiency or welfare effects.
  - *why*: Counterintuitive welfare findings (e.g., a harmful practice creating value) mark Management Science papers.
- **DROPPED**: Studies innovation as search or problem-solving behavior measured on novel behavioral data, rather than audience perceptions of novelty or distinctiveness.
  - *why*: Too narrow; covered by the behavior-data and coined-label rules, and the slot was needed for a general rule.
- **ADDED**: In psychology-style pairs, prefer effort or performance in real work groups plus lab replication over many-study packages on lay beliefs or self-reports.
  - *why*: A status-effort study using field teams and a lab lost to a seven-study beliefs package.
- **EDITED**: Contribution framed around named literatures or coined labels (a new 'paradox', a named hypothesis, optimal distinctiveness, categories) rather than a substantive answer.
  - *now*: Contribution built on a coined concept or label ('we introduce the concept of', 'what we call', a new paradox, optimal distinctiveness), even with strong design.
  - *why*: Losing abstracts in two misjudged pairs were built around a newly named concept despite strong experimental designs.
- **EDITED**: Long verbal theorizing ('we/I theorize', inverted U, lists of moderators) where the data merely confirm predicted directions without identification.
  - *now*: Long verbal theorizing ('we theorize', inverted U, moderator lists, 'self-limiting' propositions) where data merely confirm predicted directions without identification.
  - *why*: Platform data followed by post-hoc theorizing about conditions lost to a mechanism-separating experiment.
- **DROPPED**: Methodological critique of an existing measure pitched as guidance for 'organizational' or strategy scholars rather than answering a substantive question.
  - *why*: Already covered by the measure rule in values; the slot was freed for a simulation/suggestive-evidence flag.
- **ADDED**: Pure simulation models arguing against a prevailing explanation without data; or associational findings backed only by 'suggestive' mechanism evidence.
  - *why*: A data-free computational model lost to an RCT; an 'associated with'/'suggestive evidence' study lost to a validated-measure field test.
