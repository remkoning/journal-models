# Changelog v1 (revision of v0)

**REJECTED** at iteration 1: val accuracy 93.3% (vs OrgSci 93.3%, vs SMJ 93.3%); best so far 96.7%, threshold 95.7%. Revision of v0.

Train pool: v0 judged 39 train pairs, accuracy 93.6% (vs OrgSci 86.8%, vs SMJ 100.0%). Reflection saw 3 misjudged, 6 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **ADDED**: Formal models (game-theoretic, principal-agent, dynamic, market equilibrium) that derive clear results about incentives, contracts, or welfare.
  - *why*: MS articles in several pairs were stylized formal models; their counterparts were empirical or verbal theory.
- **ADDED**: Economics reasoning about mechanisms: incentives, contractibility, credibility, market failure, efficiency, collusion, or the costs and benefits of adoption.
  - *why*: The MS side used economic mechanisms such as relational contracts and collusion, while the counterparts used trust or hypothesis-testing strategy framing.
- **ADDED**: Large-scale archival, administrative, platform, census, or proprietary transaction data analysed with careful identification or natural experiments.
  - *why*: MS winners used census, platform, or search-firm data; the losers used interviews or case studies.
- **ADDED**: Precise measurement of an important phenomenon, such as how widespread, fast, or large it is, even if mostly descriptive, when it informs productivity or policy.
  - *why*: A nationally representative descriptive measurement paper beat a field experiment whose headline was a new concept.
- **ADDED**: Concrete quantified findings (percentages, effect sizes, profit or time gaps) reported directly in the abstract.
  - *why*: MS abstracts consistently report effect magnitudes.
- **ADDED**: Controlled lab or field experiments, especially when combined with computational or formal modeling to separate mechanisms.
  - *why*: Experiment plus computational modeling beat archival theorizing on the same group-learning question.
- **ADDED**: Comparing decision structures (individuals vs. groups, centralized vs. decentralized) through their effects on choices, learning, or performance.
  - *why*: MS favours a decision-science framing of how organizational structure shapes choices.
- **ADDED**: Outcomes that can be measured as behaviour or economic results (effort, hiring, profits, productivity, sourcing, market share) rather than identities or perceptions.
  - *why*: MS winners on status, gender, and hiring topics measured effort, hiring, or profit; the losers measured perceptions or identity.
- **ADDED**: Separating competing explanations or sources, such as supply vs. demand or aggregation vs. context effects, to locate where an effect really comes from.
  - *why*: MS papers separated sources of disparities and of group effects.
- **ADDED**: A model or analysis that explains empirical patterns and draws out unexpected implications for value creation or policy.
  - *why*: A historical natural experiment paired with a model showing an unexpected welfare effect beat hypothesis testing derived from game theory.
- **ADDED**: Qualitative interviews, ethnography, or a single longitudinal case study that builds a process model or new theory.
  - *why*: Interview-based and case-study counterparts were consistently the non-MS article.
- **ADDED**: Methodological commentary or recommendations aimed at researchers in one field rather than a substantive finding.
  - *why*: A methods-guidance paper for strategy research was the non-MS article.
- **ADDED**: Coining a new concept or label as the headline contribution rather than a model result or a quantified finding.
  - *why*: Leading with a new concept marked the non-MS side even when the evidence was experimental.
- **ADDED**: Abstracts mostly made of 'we argue/theorize/hypothesize' statements with no results or magnitudes reported.
  - *why*: Abstracts that were all theorizing with no findings belonged to other journals.
- **ADDED**: Framed mainly as extending a named management theory (resource dependence, behavioral theory of the firm, identity) or contributing to a literature.
  - *why*: Counterparts framed their contribution as extending a named management theory.
- **ADDED**: Main focus on how people subjectively experience status, gender, or identity, measured through felt appropriateness or beliefs.
  - *why*: Subjective-experience framings of status or gender appeared on the non-MS side.
- **ADDED**: Theorizing propositions after the fact from observational patterns ('we propose a self-limiting nature') instead of testing or modeling them.
  - *why*: Theorizing from archival patterns after the fact lost to an experimental and modeling design.
