# Changelog v4 (revision of v3)

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 4: val accuracy 96.7% (vs OrgSci 93.3%, vs SMJ 100.0%); best so far 96.7%, threshold 95.7%. Revision of v3.

Train pool: v3 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 96.2% (vs OrgSci 100.0%, vs SMJ 92.5%). Reflection saw 2 misjudged, 7 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Credible causal identification: policy shocks, before/after events, counterfactual benchmarks, instruments, or field data isolating one mechanism.
  - *now*: Credible causal identification (policy shocks, sudden events, instruments, randomization) beats panel associations with moderators, even on large samples.
  - *why*: Exogenous-shock papers beat large-sample associational panels with moderators but the board was only weakly confident.
- **EDITED**: A formal model that yields clear, checkable results, ideally paired with empirical measurement that quantifies its trade-offs.
  - *now*: A formal model (real options, matching, game, optimal choice) that derives predictions, especially when tested by field experiment or data, even if magnitudes are unstated.
  - *why*: Model-derived hypotheses tested empirically, and analytical models of optimal choice, were underweighted against archival rivals.
- **EDITED**: A new, validated, reusable measurement method (e.g., text-based measures validated several ways) that predicts real outcomes.
  - *now*: A new, validated, reusable measure that predicts real outcomes; not a critique or correction of an existing measure.
  - *why*: The measurement rule wrongly favored a critique of an existing measure over a policy-shock study.
- **ADDED**: Tests a common management practice (rankings, pay, hiring tools) on firm records, ruling out alternative mechanisms or showing standard remedies fail.
  - *why*: A single-firm personnel-data study of an evaluation practice lost out to an RCT built on a psychological mechanism.
- **ADDED**: Studies innovation as search or problem-solving behavior measured on novel behavioral data, rather than audience perceptions of novelty or distinctiveness.
  - *why*: A study of innovation search behavior was published over a study of audience valuation built on a coined hypothesis.
- **EDITED**: Contribution framed mainly as 'implications for research on' named literatures (optimal distinctiveness, ambidexterity, categories) rather than a substantive answer.
  - *now*: Contribution framed around named literatures or coined labels (a new 'paradox', a named hypothesis, optimal distinctiveness, categories) rather than a substantive answer.
  - *why*: Coined paradoxes and named hypotheses that extend sociological constructs marked the non-MS papers.
- **EDITED**: Hypotheses stated as an inverted U or a list of moderators, with no effect magnitudes or identification strategy.
  - *now*: Long verbal theorizing ('we/I theorize', inverted U, lists of moderators) where the data merely confirm predicted directions without identification.
  - *why*: Non-MS papers, even experimental ones, devoted most of the abstract to theorized chains of predictions.
- **ADDED**: Methodological critique of an existing measure pitched as guidance for 'organizational' or strategy scholars rather than answering a substantive question.
  - *why*: A methods critique aimed at organizational scholars appeared outside MS, while a substantive causal study was in MS.
