# Changelog v3 (revision of v0)

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 3: val accuracy 96.7% (vs OrgSci 93.3%, vs SMJ 100.0%); best so far 96.7%, threshold 95.7%. Revision of v0.

Train pool: v0 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 93.8% (vs OrgSci 87.5%, vs SMJ 100.0%). Reflection saw 4 misjudged, 5 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **ADDED**: Economics-style mechanisms such as incentives, information asymmetry, signaling, matching, equilibrium, or explicit trade-offs, rather than legitimacy, identity, or institutional logics.
  - *why*: MS winners framed things through cheap talk, matching, and incentives; the losing abstracts leaned on legitimacy, institutional, and category theory.
- **ADDED**: Reports effect sizes in interpretable units (percentages, per-unit responses, gap closed), not just support for hypotheses' direction.
  - *why*: MS abstracts often stated quantified magnitudes; the other journals' abstracts reported only whether predictions were supported.
- **ADDED**: Credible causal identification: policy shocks, before/after events, counterfactual benchmarks, instruments, or field data isolating one mechanism.
  - *why*: MS winners used shocks, award receipt events, counterfactual matches, and IVs.
- **ADDED**: A formal model that yields clear, checkable results, ideally paired with empirical measurement that quantifies its trade-offs.
  - *why*: MS favored a model plus data, and parsimonious equilibrium models, over a simulation-only portfolio model.
- **ADDED**: A new, validated, reusable measurement method (e.g., text-based measures validated several ways) that predicts real outcomes.
  - *why*: Several MS winners contributed new measurement approaches as their core contribution.
- **ADDED**: Large-sample archival, administrative, platform, or field data on actual behavior over small samples, vignettes, or single cases.
  - *why*: MS winners relied on comprehensive behavioral data; the losers relied on cases, vignettes, or narrow samples.
- **ADDED**: Shows how decision makers (firms, employers, evaluators, boards) actually respond to constraints, with concrete managerial or policy implications.
  - *why*: MS winners emphasized measurable responses by firms and evaluators to constraints.
- **ADDED**: Precise, decision-relevant formalization of fuzzy strategy concepts that produces tools usable in empirical work.
  - *why*: A formalization of competitive intensity beat a case study; it was published in MS.
- **ADDED**: When both abstracts are sociological, prefer the one testing behavioral outcomes on large archival data over qualitative or multimethod case work.
  - *why*: The board tied on a status-inconsistency paper versus an authenticity case-plus-experiment paper, and the archival one was MS.
- **ADDED**: Questions about bias, discrimination, or inequality framed as testable demand-side or incentive mechanisms rather than as contextualizing institutional forces.
  - *why*: In the gender-gap and evaluator-bias pairs, MS took the mechanism-testing versions.
- **ADDED**: Inductive qualitative work: grounded theory, single in-depth case studies, ethnography, or process models built from interviews.
  - *why*: Case and grounded-theory abstracts consistently appeared elsewhere.
- **ADDED**: Contribution framed mainly as 'implications for research on' named literatures (optimal distinctiveness, ambidexterity, categories) rather than a substantive answer.
  - *why*: The losing abstracts closed by listing the literatures they contribute to.
- **ADDED**: Hypotheses stated as an inverted U or a list of moderators, with no effect magnitudes or identification strategy.
  - *why*: The inverted-U multi-project paper and the moderator-heavy exemplar paper were from other journals.
- **ADDED**: Formal models full of coined jargon constructs and long lists of propositions, without a crisp economic insight or empirical tie-in.
  - *why*: A jargon-heavy CEO succession model lost to an empirical directors paper, so being a formal model is not enough by itself.
- **ADDED**: Purely conceptual critique or redefinition of a construct with only verbal or illustrative arguments.
  - *why*: A conceptual competitive-advantage measure paper appeared outside MS.
- **ADDED**: Context-bound descriptive findings (one occupation or sector) framed as institutional or contextual nuance rather than a general mechanism.
  - *why*: The contextualized gender pay study and the status-decoupling study lost to mechanism-focused counterparts.
- **ADDED**: Multimethod designs that combine a case narrative with hypothetical-vignette experiments to illustrate a perception mechanism.
  - *why*: A case-plus-experiment authenticity paper was published elsewhere.
