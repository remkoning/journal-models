# Changelog v6 (revision of v4)

**REJECTED** at iteration 6: val accuracy 90.0% (vs OrgSci 78.9%, vs SMJ 100.0%); best so far 92.5%, threshold 91.5%. Revision of v4.

Train pool: v4 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 95.0% (vs OrgSci 90.0%, vs SMJ 100.0%). Reflection saw 2 misjudged, 7 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Archival, administrative, platform, or proprietary data with exogenous variation (policy shocks, sudden events, unexpected deaths, strict cutoffs, instruments) to identify causal effects.
  - *now*: Large archival, administrative, platform, or proprietary data with exogenous variation (policy shocks, sudden deaths, random or quasi-random assignment, cutoffs, instruments).
  - *why*: Winners used random or quasi-random assignment by firms or institutions and very large populations; I made those explicit.
- **EDITED**: Controlled lab or field experiments, especially when combined with computational or formal modeling to separate mechanisms.
  - *now*: Field experiments or quasi-random assignment inside real organizations; lab experiments mainly when incentivized and paired with formal or computational modeling.
  - *why*: Lab and real-effort experiments without models repeatedly lost to field experiments or archival designs.
- **EDITED**: Outcomes that can be measured as behaviour or economic results (effort, exit, hiring, profits, productivity, sourcing, market share) rather than identities or perceptions.
  - *now*: Outcomes that can be measured as behaviour or economic results (effort, exit, hiring, capital raised, revenues, productivity, violations) rather than identities or perceptions.
  - *why*: Broadened the generic outcome examples to cover financing, sales and compliance outcomes that winning abstracts used.
- **EDITED**: Separating competing explanations or sources, such as supply vs. demand or team vs. individual effects, to locate where an effect really comes from.
  - *now*: Separating competing explanations or sources (supply vs. demand, team vs. individual, detection vs. behavior) via decomposition, fixed effects, or targeted tests.
  - *why*: Winners ruled out rival channels through statistical decomposition; the board needed to credit this even alongside a coined construct.
- **ADDED**: Biases or information frictions in evaluators, consumers, or monitors documented in real-world decisions and market data, not hypothetical scenarios.
  - *why*: Several winners showed evaluator or monitoring frictions in field or platform data, while the losers relied on hypothetical or post hoc theorizing.
- **EDITED**: Coining a new concept, paradox, or named hypothesis as the headline contribution rather than a model result or a quantified finding.
  - *now*: Coining a new concept, paradox, or named hypothesis as the headline contribution, when not backed by causal identification or large-scale behavioral evidence.
  - *why*: The board wrongly penalized a winner that introduced a construct but backed it with an instrument and decomposition.
- **EDITED**: Framed mainly as extending a named management theory or literature, with neither a formal model nor a causal design to anchor it.
  - *now*: Framed mainly as extending a named management theory (routines, path dependence, legitimacy) or bridging literatures, rather than resolving an economic question.
  - *why*: Losing abstracts framed themselves as bridging literatures or invoked organizational-theory mechanisms, even when they had a natural experiment.
- **EDITED**: Main focus on subjective experience (identity, status, felt fit, stress, wellbeing) as the outcome or headline mechanism rather than behaviour or incentives.
  - *now*: Main focus on subjective experience (identity, status, felt fit, authenticity, stress) as the outcome or headline mechanism rather than behaviour or incentives.
  - *why*: Perceived authenticity is another subjective-perception headline typical of the other journals.
- **EDITED**: Correlational panel results ('is associated with') stacked with moderators or moderated mediation, with no exogenous variation addressing endogeneity.
  - *now*: Headline result is 'it depends': effects contingent on stage, structure, or hierarchy via interactions in correlational data without exogenous variation.
  - *why*: The board chose a contingency-moderator abstract over an identified one; this red flag needed to be sharper.
- **ADDED**: Vignette or multi-study lab experiments measuring hypothetical perceptions or evaluations, rather than real choices with stakes in field settings.
  - *why*: Hypothetical perception experiments and multi-study lab designs lost to field and platform-data counterparts.
