# Changelog v10 (revision of v7)

**REJECTED** at iteration 10: val accuracy 91.2% (vs OrgSci 81.6%, vs SMJ 100.0%); best so far 92.5%, threshold 91.5%. Revision of v7.

Train pool: v7 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 96.2% (vs OrgSci 92.5%, vs SMJ 100.0%). Reflection saw 2 misjudged, 7 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Economics reasoning about mechanisms: incentives, signaling, cheap talk, information asymmetry, credibility, contractibility, market failure, efficiency, or costs and benefits of adoption.
  - *now*: Economics reasoning about mechanisms: incentives, signaling, information asymmetry or frictions, discrimination, credibility, contractibility, market failure, or adoption costs and benefits.
  - *why*: Management Science winners framed problems as market frictions such as consumer discrimination or mismatches between producer claims and audience perception; these need to count as economics mechanisms.
- **EDITED**: Archival, administrative, platform, or proprietary data with exogenous variation (policy shocks, sudden events, cutoffs, instruments) to identify causal effects.
  - *now*: Archival, historical, administrative, or platform data with exogenous variation (policy shocks, unexpected deaths, instruments, random or quasi-random assignment); such identification outweighs framing or construct labels.
  - *why*: Several Management Science winners hinged on an identification device (historical-law instrument, deaths, firm randomization, quasi-random evaluators), yet the board let construct framing outweigh it.
- **EDITED**: Randomized lab, field, or platform experiments on behaviour, ideally preregistered, which count as stronger evidence than quasi-experiments with long causal chains.
  - *now*: Randomized field experiments inside real firms or markets with consequential outcomes (turnover, sales, misconduct records); these beat lab or online studies of stylized tasks.
  - *why*: Lab, vignette, and online real-effort experiments repeatedly appeared on the non-Management Science side, while field experiments in real firms appeared on the Management Science side.
- **EDITED**: Outcomes measured as behaviour or economic results (effort, exit, hiring, applications, ratings, profits, productivity, offshoring, market share) rather than identities or perceptions.
  - *now*: Outcomes measured as revealed behaviour or economic results (effort, exit, hiring, user ratings, revenue, capital raised, productivity) across many actors, rather than identities or perceptions.
  - *why*: Large-scale revealed behaviour of platform users or markets separated Management Science papers from same-topic studies built on stylized evaluations.
- **EDITED**: Coining a new concept, paradox, or labelled construct as the headline contribution, even when it is wrapped in a formal model with many propositions.
  - *now*: Coining a new concept, paradox, or labelled construct as the headline when it is only illustrated, not tested with large-sample data or causal identification.
  - *why*: A Management Science paper introducing a new construct, backed by an instrument and large data, was misjudged because this flag penalized any construct-coining.
- **EDITED**: Contributions aimed at named literatures (categories, legitimacy, optimal distinctiveness, behavioral theory of the firm, strain theory) without a formal model or causal design.
  - *now*: Arguments aimed at named literatures (legitimacy, authenticity, optimal distinctiveness, behavioral theory) tested only with small lab, vignette, or survey studies of hypothetical evaluations.
  - *why*: Category research tested on large platform data was published in Management Science, while paradox-style arguments tested with small lab or vignette experiments went elsewhere.
- **EDITED**: Correlational panel results ('is associated with') stacked with moderators or moderated mediation, with no exogenous variation addressing endogeneity.
  - *now*: Correlational results ('is associated with') whose headline is moderators or effects flipping across life stages, hierarchy levels, or structures, with no exogenous variation.
  - *why*: Non-Management Science papers headlined effects that reverse across stages or hierarchy levels without identification; the old rule caught this too weakly.
