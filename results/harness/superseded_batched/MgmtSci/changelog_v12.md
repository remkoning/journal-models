# Changelog v12 (revision of v7)

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 12: val accuracy 92.5% (vs OrgSci 84.2%, vs SMJ 100.0%); best so far 92.5%, threshold 91.5%. Revision of v7.

Train pool: v7 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 100.0% (vs OrgSci 100.0%, vs SMJ 100.0%). Reflection saw 0 misjudged, 9 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Formal or economic models (game theory, principal-agent, matching, search, equilibrium) with clear results, best when their predictions are then tested on large-sample data.
  - *now*: Formal or economic models (game theory, principal-agent, matching, search, equilibrium) with clear results; pure analytical models with optimal choices count, and data tests add strength.
  - *why*: A pure analytical model with no data was only weakly preferred over a labelled-paradox empirical paper; untested models should still count strongly.
- **EDITED**: Archival, administrative, platform, or proprietary data with exogenous variation (policy shocks, sudden events, cutoffs, instruments) to identify causal effects.
  - *now*: Exogenous variation (policy shocks, sudden events, cutoffs, instruments) in archival or proprietary data counts as causal design even when results are worded 'associated with'.
  - *why*: Sudden-shock designs with hedged wording were preferred only weakly over plain correlational panels.
- **EDITED**: New quantitative measures or computational tools (text-based, clustering, validated instruments) that make a hard-to-observe construct measurable at scale and are applied to data.
  - *now*: New quantitative measures or computational tools (text-based, machine-learning linking, validated instruments) that make a hard-to-observe construct measurable at scale and are applied to data.
  - *why*: Machine-learning record linkage feeding a causal design is a typical valued tool.
- **EDITED**: Comparing decision structures (individuals vs. groups, centralized vs. decentralized, search strategies) through their effects on choices, learning, or performance.
  - *now*: Comparing decision structures or processes (individuals vs. groups, centralized vs. decentralized, search order or strategy) through their effects on choices, learning, or performance.
  - *why*: Comparisons of search processes and their innovation outcomes were underweighted against perception-based valuation studies.
- **ADDED**: Empirical work organized by an established economic framework (two-sided matching, assortative sorting, search) beats framework-free relationship mapping, even when results are only suggestive.
  - *why*: Matching-framework empirics with modest results were nearly lost to large-sample descriptive relationship studies.
- **EDITED**: Methodological commentary or best-practice advice to researchers; this does not apply to new formal or computational methods actually applied to data.
  - *now*: Methodological commentary for researchers, including critiques of an existing measure's error with proposed fixes; does not apply to new methods answering a substantive question.
  - *why*: Measurement-error critiques with fixes for scholars slipped through the old exception.
- **EDITED**: Coining a new concept, paradox, or labelled construct as the headline contribution, even when it is wrapped in a formal model with many propositions.
  - *now*: Coining a new concept, paradox, or labelled hypothesis as the headline contribution, even when tested with field data, an experiment, or a formal model.
  - *why*: Labelled hypotheses or paradoxes backed by field and experimental tests still marked the non-MS abstract.
- **EDITED**: Abstracts dominated by 'we argue/theorize/propose' statements without reported results or magnitudes, or propositions inferred post hoc from observed patterns.
  - *now*: Abstracts dominated by 'we argue/theorize/posit' chains, with data confirming only one piece or reported without results or magnitudes.
  - *why*: Long theorized multi-stage chains confirmed by one experimental number lost to clean, question-led experiments.
- **EDITED**: Contributions aimed at named literatures (categories, legitimacy, optimal distinctiveness, behavioral theory of the firm, strain theory) without a formal model or causal design.
  - *now*: Contributions aimed at named literatures (categories, legitimacy, optimal distinctiveness, embeddedness, behavioral theory of the firm) without a formal model or causal design.
  - *why*: Sociological embeddedness framing is a recurring marker of non-MS outlets.
- **EDITED**: Subjective experience (identity, status, felt fit, trust, stress, wellbeing) as the outcome or headline mechanism, rather than behaviour or incentives.
  - *now*: Subjective experience (identity, status, job satisfaction, perceived value, trust, stress, mental health) as the outcome or headline mechanism, rather than behaviour or incentives.
  - *why*: Job satisfaction outcomes and mental-health mechanisms marked non-MS abstracts but were only weakly penalized.
