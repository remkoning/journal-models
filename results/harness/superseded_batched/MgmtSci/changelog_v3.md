# Changelog v3 (revision of v1)

**REJECTED** at iteration 3: val accuracy 90.0% (vs OrgSci 78.9%, vs SMJ 100.0%); best so far 92.5%, threshold 91.5%. Revision of v1.

Train pool: v1 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 100.0% (vs OrgSci 100.0%, vs SMJ 100.0%). Reflection saw 0 misjudged, 9 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Formal models (game-theoretic, principal-agent, dynamic, market equilibrium) that derive clear results about incentives, contracts, or welfare.
  - *now*: Formal models deriving clear results on incentives, contracts, or welfare—strongest when paired with data that measures the model's predicted trade-offs.
  - *why*: In several pairs, model-only or simulation papers lost to empirical papers or to papers combining a model with data; the old rule over-rewarded models alone.
- **EDITED**: Economics reasoning about mechanisms: incentives, contractibility, credibility, market failure, efficiency, collusion, or the costs and benefits of adoption.
  - *now*: Economics reasoning about mechanisms: incentives, information asymmetry, signaling or cheap talk, screening, contractibility, credibility, market failure, efficiency, or adoption costs and benefits.
  - *why*: The winning papers used information-economics framing (cheap talk, screening, demand-side) where the losing papers used sociological legitimacy framing.
- **ADDED**: New validated quantitative measures, indexes, or computational methods that reveal phenomena existing measures cannot detect, applied to real data.
  - *why*: Papers introducing new measurement indexes or computational frameworks won but were nearly misjudged as 'methodological'.
- **ADDED**: Gender or racial inequality studied through hard outcomes (pay, promotion, turnover, ratings) with identification strategies and reported magnitudes.
  - *why*: Gender and race papers with identified, quantified behavioral outcomes won, but the board was wary of them because of the identity red flag.
- **EDITED**: Methodological commentary or recommendations aimed at researchers in one field rather than a substantive finding.
  - *now*: Methodological commentary, reviews, or recommendations to researchers that offer neither a new measurement tool nor a substantive finding.
  - *why*: The old flag wrongly penalized papers whose contribution is a new quantitative measure or method.
- **EDITED**: Framed mainly as extending a named management theory (resource dependence, behavioral theory of the firm, identity) or contributing to a literature.
  - *now*: Framed mainly as extending a named organizational theory (behavioral theory of the firm, institutional, legitimacy, categories/optimal distinctiveness, identity) or contributing to its literature.
  - *why*: The losing papers framed contributions as extending institutional, category, or optimal-distinctiveness literatures.
- **EDITED**: Main focus on how people subjectively experience status, gender, or identity, measured through felt appropriateness or beliefs.
  - *now*: Main focus on subjective perceptions or felt experiences of status, gender, or identity rather than measured behavioral or economic outcomes.
  - *why*: Narrowed so the flag hits perception-focused work, not gender or status studies measured through behavior or pay.
- **ADDED**: Stand-alone theory models or simulations offering propositions or 'testable predictions' with no data, when the rival brings well-identified empirical evidence.
  - *why*: Pure model or simulation papers with propositions repeatedly lost to rigorous empirical counterparts on the same topic.
