# Changelog v8 (revision of v7)

**REJECTED** at iteration 8: val accuracy 88.8% (vs OrgSci 81.6%, vs SMJ 95.2%); best so far 92.5%, threshold 91.5%. Revision of v7.

Train pool: v7 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 100.0% (vs OrgSci 100.0%, vs SMJ 100.0%). Reflection saw 0 misjudged, 9 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Formal or economic models (game theory, principal-agent, matching, search, equilibrium) with clear results, best when their predictions are then tested on large-sample data.
  - *now*: Formal or economic models (game theory, principal-agent, matching, search, equilibrium) with characterized optimal choices; pure analytical models count fully even without data.
  - *why*: Board hesitated on pure analytical models and matching frameworks that beat empirically richer non-MS counterparts.
- **EDITED**: Randomized lab, field, or platform experiments on behaviour, ideally preregistered, which count as stronger evidence than quasi-experiments with long causal chains.
  - *now*: Randomized lab, field, or platform experiments with a sharp question and a headline behavioural or economic magnitude, ideally preregistered.
  - *why*: The claim that RCTs beat quasi-experiments led the board to favour an RCT with a wellbeing mechanism over a cutoff-based behavioural study.
- **EDITED**: New quantitative measures or computational tools (text-based, clustering, validated instruments) that make a hard-to-observe construct measurable at scale and are applied to data.
  - *now*: New measures or computational tools (text, machine-learning linkage, clustering) used to deliver substantive findings about a phenomenon, not merely to critique existing measures.
  - *why*: Papers that critique a measure and propose fixes were drawing credit that belongs to papers producing substantive findings.
- **EDITED**: Comparing decision structures (individuals vs. groups, centralized vs. decentralized, search strategies) through their effects on choices, learning, or performance.
  - *now*: Comparing decision structures or problem-solving processes (individuals vs. groups, centralized vs. decentralized, search order or strategy) through effects on choices, innovations, or performance.
  - *why*: Studies comparing search sequences on real innovation outputs were MS, but the board underweighted them.
- **EDITED**: Outcomes measured as behaviour or economic results (effort, exit, hiring, applications, ratings, profits, productivity, offshoring, market share) rather than identities or perceptions.
  - *now*: Outcomes measured as behaviour or economic results (effort, exit, hiring, applications, salary demands, profits, productivity, innovation output) rather than identities or perceptions.
  - *why*: Generalize the examples toward the labour and innovation outcomes common in MS winners.
- **EDITED**: Methodological commentary or best-practice advice to researchers; this does not apply to new formal or computational methods actually applied to data.
  - *now*: Methodological commentary aimed at researchers, including critiques of an existing measure's error with proposed fixes, when no new substantive finding results.
  - *why*: The old exception let a measurement-error critique with fixes look like a valued new method.
- **EDITED**: Coining a new concept, paradox, or labelled construct as the headline contribution, even when it is wrapped in a formal model with many propositions.
  - *now*: Coining a new concept, paradox, or named hypothesis as the headline contribution, even when supported by a formal model, field data, or experiments.
  - *why*: Labelled hypotheses and paradoxes lost even when backed by field data or experiments; the board gave them too much credit.
- **EDITED**: Contributions aimed at named literatures (categories, legitimacy, optimal distinctiveness, behavioral theory of the firm, strain theory) without a formal model or causal design.
  - *now*: Contributions aimed at named sociological or organizational literatures (categories, legitimacy, embeddedness, social comparison, optimal distinctiveness) without a formal model or causal design.
  - *why*: Embeddedness and social-comparison framings also marked non-MS abstracts.
- **EDITED**: Subjective experience (identity, status, felt fit, trust, stress, wellbeing) as the outcome or headline mechanism, rather than behaviour or incentives.
  - *now*: Subjective experience (identity, status, satisfaction, trust, stress, mental health, wellbeing) as outcome or headline mechanism, even inside a randomized experiment.
  - *why*: Job satisfaction and mental-health mechanisms marked non-MS abstracts even when the design was strong.
