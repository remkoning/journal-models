# Changelog v4 (revision of v1)

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 4: val accuracy 92.5% (vs OrgSci 84.2%, vs SMJ 100.0%); best so far 92.5%, threshold 91.5%. Revision of v1.

Train pool: v1 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 97.5% (vs OrgSci 100.0%, vs SMJ 95.0%). Reflection saw 1 misjudged, 8 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Formal models (game-theoretic, principal-agent, dynamic, market equilibrium) that derive clear results about incentives, contracts, or welfare.
  - *now*: Formal or economic frameworks (game-theoretic, principal-agent, matching, search, market equilibrium) that derive clear results about incentives, contracts, sorting, or welfare.
  - *why*: Two-sided matching frameworks marked the Management Science paper, but the old rule did not cover matching or sorting.
- **EDITED**: Large-scale archival, administrative, platform, census, or proprietary transaction data analysed with careful identification or natural experiments.
  - *now*: Archival, administrative, platform, or proprietary data with exogenous variation (policy shocks, sudden events, unexpected deaths, strict cutoffs, instruments) to identify causal effects.
  - *why*: Management Science winners relied on policy shocks, cutoffs, and instruments; the losing papers had associational panels of similar size.
- **EDITED**: Comparing decision structures (individuals vs. groups, centralized vs. decentralized) through their effects on choices, learning, or performance.
  - *now*: Comparing decision structures (individuals vs. groups, centralized vs. decentralized, search strategies) through their effects on choices, learning, or performance.
  - *why*: Comparing search processes and their effect on innovation outcomes fits the same structural-comparison taste.
- **EDITED**: Outcomes that can be measured as behaviour or economic results (effort, hiring, profits, productivity, sourcing, market share) rather than identities or perceptions.
  - *now*: Outcomes that can be measured as behaviour or economic results (effort, exit, hiring, profits, productivity, sourcing, market share) rather than identities or perceptions.
  - *why*: Voluntary exit is a key behavioural outcome in workforce papers; added it as a generic example.
- **EDITED**: Separating competing explanations or sources, such as supply vs. demand or aggregation vs. context effects, to locate where an effect really comes from.
  - *now*: Separating competing explanations or sources, such as supply vs. demand or team vs. individual effects, to locate where an effect really comes from.
  - *why*: Attributing outcomes to team-level rather than individual-level factors was a distinguishing feature, so I used a more general example.
- **ADDED**: Testing whether a common management practice or policy works, including its unintended consequences and why standard remedies fail.
  - *why*: The misjudged pair: Management Science favoured a paper evaluating a widespread practice's unintended effects and the failure of countermeasures.
- **EDITED**: Coining a new concept or label as the headline contribution rather than a model result or a quantified finding.
  - *now*: Coining a new concept, paradox, or named hypothesis as the headline contribution rather than a model result or a quantified finding.
  - *why*: The losing papers headlined labelled paradoxes and named hypotheses, not just new concepts.
- **EDITED**: Framed mainly as extending a named management theory (resource dependence, behavioral theory of the firm, identity) or contributing to a literature.
  - *now*: Framed mainly as extending a named management theory or literature, with neither a formal model nor a causal design to anchor it.
  - *why*: Management Science model papers also claim to enrich literatures; the flag should apply only when no model or causal design backs the claim.
- **EDITED**: Main focus on how people subjectively experience status, gender, or identity, measured through felt appropriateness or beliefs.
  - *now*: Main focus on subjective experience (identity, status, felt fit, stress, wellbeing) as the outcome or headline mechanism rather than behaviour or incentives.
  - *why*: The non-Management Science workforce paper rested its main mechanism on stress and mental health.
- **ADDED**: Correlational panel results ('is associated with') stacked with moderators or moderated mediation, with no exogenous variation addressing endogeneity.
  - *why*: Several losing papers used moderated associations without identification, while their counterparts exploited shocks or instruments.
