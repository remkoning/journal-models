# Changelog v9 (revision of v7)

**REJECTED** at iteration 9: val accuracy 91.2% (vs OrgSci 81.6%, vs SMJ 100.0%); best so far 92.5%, threshold 91.5%. Revision of v7.

Train pool: v7 judged 39 train pairs, accuracy 97.4% (vs OrgSci 94.7%, vs SMJ 100.0%). Reflection saw 1 misjudged, 8 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Formal or economic models (game theory, principal-agent, matching, search, equilibrium) with clear results, best when their predictions are then tested on large-sample data.
  - *now*: Formal or economic models (game theory, principal-agent, matching, search, equilibrium) with analytically characterized optima or equilibria; these count fully even without data.
  - *why*: A pure principal-agent model with a characterized optimal process was MS but got only weak support against a field experiment.
- **EDITED**: Economics reasoning about mechanisms: incentives, signaling, cheap talk, information asymmetry, credibility, contractibility, market failure, efficiency, or costs and benefits of adoption.
  - *now*: Economics reasoning about mechanisms: incentives, observability of effort, signaling, cheap talk, information asymmetry, credibility, contractibility, self-enforcement, market failure, efficiency, or adoption costs.
  - *why*: MS winners turned on effort visibility and self-enforcing relational contracts; the rule should name these economic mechanisms explicitly.
- **EDITED**: Randomized lab, field, or platform experiments on behaviour, ideally preregistered, which count as stronger evidence than quasi-experiments with long causal chains.
  - *now*: Randomized lab, field, or platform experiments, including RCTs testing training or decision practices among firms or entrepreneurs; ideally preregistered or paired with fitted behavioural models.
  - *why*: An RCT on startup decision practices beat a simulation, and an experiment with computational modelling beat observational theorizing; both were judged with low confidence.
- **EDITED**: Outcomes measured as behaviour or economic results (effort, exit, hiring, applications, ratings, profits, productivity, offshoring, market share) rather than identities or perceptions.
  - *now*: Outcomes measured as behaviour or economic results (effort, exit, hiring, applications, profits, productivity, market share); perceived mediators are fine when the outcome is behaviour.
  - *why*: An MS paper whose outcome was effort, with performance expectations as the mediator, was misjudged because of its psychological framing.
- **ADDED**: When both abstracts are psychological, favour the one whose moderator is an observable task or incentive condition (visibility, monitoring) over individuals' beliefs.
  - *why*: Tie-breaker for multi-study micro pairs: the misjudged MS paper used a structural moderator, while the rival used a belief-based construct.
- **EDITED**: Coining a new concept, paradox, or labelled construct as the headline contribution, even when it is wrapped in a formal model with many propositions.
  - *now*: Coining a new concept, paradox, or labelled construct as the headline contribution, even when it is wrapped in a formal model or experiment.
  - *why*: Non-MS rivals coined labelled constructs even while running strong multi-study or field experiments.
- **EDITED**: Subjective experience (identity, status, felt fit, trust, stress, wellbeing) as the outcome or headline mechanism, rather than behaviour or incentives.
  - *now*: Subjective experience (identity, status beliefs, felt fit, trust, stress, wellbeing) as the outcome or headline construct, rather than behaviour or incentives.
  - *why*: Narrowed so that status as a position predicting effort is not penalized; status beliefs as the headline construct still are.
- **EDITED**: Correlational panel results ('is associated with') stacked with moderators or moderated mediation, with no exogenous variation addressing endogeneity.
  - *now*: Correlational panel results ('is associated with') with moderators and no exogenous variation; huge samples or machine-learning control selection do not substitute for identification.
  - *why*: A million-firm study with ML-selected controls nearly beat an MS paper built on a relational-contract mechanism.
- **ADDED**: Simulation models whose outputs 'suggest' patterns to reinterpret a scholarly debate, without analytic results or data, lose to RCTs or solved models.
  - *why*: A computational simulation challenging a behavioural-bias consensus was wrongly favoured over an RCT.
