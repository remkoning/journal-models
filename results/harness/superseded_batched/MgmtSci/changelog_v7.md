# Changelog v7 (revision of v4)

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 7: val accuracy 92.5% (vs OrgSci 86.8%, vs SMJ 97.6%); best so far 92.5%, threshold 91.5%. Revision of v4.

Train pool: v4 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 98.8% (vs OrgSci 97.5%, vs SMJ 100.0%). Reflection saw 1 misjudged, 8 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Formal or economic frameworks (game-theoretic, principal-agent, matching, search, market equilibrium) that derive clear results about incentives, contracts, sorting, or welfare.
  - *now*: Formal or economic models (game theory, principal-agent, matching, search, equilibrium) with clear results, best when their predictions are then tested on large-sample data.
  - *why*: A model-plus-empirical-test paper beat a verbal-theory quasi-experiment; merged the old 'model explains patterns' rule into this one.
- **DROPPED**: A model or analysis that explains empirical patterns and draws out unexpected implications for value creation or policy.
  - *why*: Merged into the formal-model rule to free a slot and avoid overlap.
- **EDITED**: Economics reasoning about mechanisms: incentives, contractibility, credibility, market failure, efficiency, collusion, or the costs and benefits of adoption.
  - *now*: Economics reasoning about mechanisms: incentives, signaling, cheap talk, information asymmetry, credibility, contractibility, market failure, efficiency, or costs and benefits of adoption.
  - *why*: The misjudged pair's winner framed claims as cheap talk under information asymmetry, while the loser used category/legitimacy theory.
- **EDITED**: Precise measurement of an important phenomenon, such as how widespread, fast, or large it is, even if mostly descriptive, when it informs productivity or policy.
  - *now*: Precise, comprehensive measurement of an important phenomenon (prevalence, speed, size), even if mostly descriptive, when it informs productivity, governance, or policy.
  - *why*: A 'first comprehensive' large cross-country measurement of directors beat a purely theoretical model.
- **EDITED**: Concrete quantified findings (percentages, effect sizes, profit or time gaps) reported directly in the abstract.
  - *now*: Concrete quantified findings in the abstract: percentages, effect sizes, per-unit responses, share of a gap closed, profit or time differences.
  - *why*: Winners reported per-unit responses and the share of a gap closed; the board underweighted these.
- **EDITED**: Controlled lab or field experiments, especially when combined with computational or formal modeling to separate mechanisms.
  - *now*: Randomized lab, field, or platform experiments on behaviour, ideally preregistered, which count as stronger evidence than quasi-experiments with long causal chains.
  - *why*: A preregistered platform field experiment beat a distant natural-event design; the board leaned the wrong way.
- **ADDED**: New quantitative measures or computational tools (text-based, clustering, validated instruments) that make a hard-to-observe construct measurable at scale and are applied to data.
  - *why*: Several winners were new measurement or computational methods (text distance, latent-coalition clustering, validated claim measures).
- **EDITED**: Outcomes that can be measured as behaviour or economic results (effort, exit, hiring, profits, productivity, sourcing, market share) rather than identities or perceptions.
  - *now*: Outcomes measured as behaviour or economic results (effort, exit, hiring, applications, ratings, profits, productivity, offshoring, market share) rather than identities or perceptions.
  - *why*: Broadened the generic list of behavioural outcomes seen in the winners.
- **EDITED**: Separating competing explanations or sources, such as supply vs. demand or team vs. individual effects, to locate where an effect really comes from.
  - *now*: Separating competing explanations or decomposing an effect, such as supply vs. demand or which observations drive it, to locate where it comes from.
  - *why*: A winner broke the effect down to show which ratings drove it; added decomposition explicitly.
- **EDITED**: Testing whether a common management practice or policy works, including its unintended consequences and why standard remedies fail.
  - *now*: Testing whether a common management practice or policy works, including unintended consequences, firm workarounds, and why standard remedies fail.
  - *why*: A winner studied how firms get around a restrictive policy, with a quantified response.
- **EDITED**: Qualitative interviews, ethnography, or a single longitudinal case study that builds a process model or new theory.
  - *now*: Qualitative interviews, ethnography, or single in-depth case studies that build a process model or new theory, even when paired with small experiments.
  - *why*: A case-plus-experiment multimethod paper lost to an archival study.
- **EDITED**: Methodological commentary or recommendations aimed at researchers in one field rather than a substantive finding.
  - *now*: Methodological commentary or best-practice advice to researchers; this does not apply to new formal or computational methods actually applied to data.
  - *why*: A new computational framework won, so the old rule risked penalizing it.
- **EDITED**: Coining a new concept, paradox, or named hypothesis as the headline contribution rather than a model result or a quantified finding.
  - *now*: Coining a new concept, paradox, or labelled construct as the headline contribution, even when it is wrapped in a formal model with many propositions.
  - *why*: A formal model built around a newly coined construct lost to a large-sample IV study.
- **EDITED**: Abstracts mostly made of 'we argue/theorize/hypothesize' statements with no results or magnitudes reported.
  - *now*: Abstracts dominated by 'we argue/theorize/propose' statements without reported results or magnitudes, or propositions inferred post hoc from observed patterns.
  - *why*: Merged the post-hoc-propositions rule here to free a slot.
- **DROPPED**: Theorizing propositions after the fact from observational patterns ('we propose a self-limiting nature') instead of testing or modeling them.
  - *why*: Folded into the 'we argue' red flag.
- **EDITED**: Framed mainly as extending a named management theory or literature, with neither a formal model nor a causal design to anchor it.
  - *now*: Contributions aimed at named literatures (categories, legitimacy, optimal distinctiveness, behavioral theory of the firm, strain theory) without a formal model or causal design.
  - *why*: Losers repeatedly claimed 'implications for research on' sociological audience-evaluation literatures; naming the lenses makes the rule easier to check.
- **EDITED**: Main focus on subjective experience (identity, status, felt fit, stress, wellbeing) as the outcome or headline mechanism rather than behaviour or incentives.
  - *now*: Subjective experience (identity, status, felt fit, trust, stress, wellbeing) as the outcome or headline mechanism, rather than behaviour or incentives.
  - *why*: A loser tested trust as the headline mechanism; added trust to the list.
- **ADDED**: Mechanism claims that rest on speculative heterogeneity splits ('hint at', 'suggest') rather than direct tests or quantified economic responses.
  - *why*: An IV study whose mechanism was only hinted at through heterogeneity lost to one with a clear quantified firm response.
