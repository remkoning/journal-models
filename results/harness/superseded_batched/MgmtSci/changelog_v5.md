# Changelog v5 (revision of v4)

**REJECTED** at iteration 5: val accuracy 91.2% (vs OrgSci 84.2%, vs SMJ 97.6%); best so far 92.5%, threshold 91.5%. Revision of v4.

Train pool: v4 judged 39 train pairs, accuracy 98.7% (vs OrgSci 97.4%, vs SMJ 100.0%). Reflection saw 1 misjudged, 8 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Formal or economic frameworks (game-theoretic, principal-agent, matching, search, market equilibrium) that derive clear results about incentives, contracts, sorting, or welfare.
  - *now*: Analytic formal models (game-theoretic, principal-agent, matching, search, equilibrium) that characterize optimal policies or equilibria and derive clear results on incentives or welfare.
  - *why*: The analytic principal-agent model beat a field experiment, while a computational simulation lost; the rule should favour analytic characterization specifically.
- **EDITED**: Economics reasoning about mechanisms: incentives, contractibility, credibility, market failure, efficiency, collusion, or the costs and benefits of adoption.
  - *now*: Economics reasoning about mechanisms: incentives, information asymmetry, signaling or cheap talk, noncontractibility, self-enforcing contracts, credibility, market failure, collusion, or efficiency.
  - *why*: MS winners framed questions as cheap talk under information asymmetry and self-enforcing relational contracts; the losers used trust or reputation constructs.
- **ADDED**: New measurement built for the question (original surveys, mystery shoppers, text-based measures validated experimentally) that makes a hard-to-observe variable testable.
  - *why*: Several MS winners stood out for purpose-built measurement instruments rather than off-the-shelf panels.
- **EDITED**: Controlled lab or field experiments, especially when combined with computational or formal modeling to separate mechanisms.
  - *now*: Randomized lab or field experiments testing an intervention or decision approach on real firms or workers, especially combined with formal modeling of mechanisms.
  - *why*: An RCT on startups beat a computational model; the old wording rewarded computational modeling too generously.
- **EDITED**: Outcomes that can be measured as behaviour or economic results (effort, exit, hiring, profits, productivity, sourcing, market share) rather than identities or perceptions.
  - *now*: Outcomes measured as behaviour or economic results (effort, exit, hiring, profits, sourcing, market share), even in psychology studies, rather than beliefs or perceptions.
  - *why*: In a status-psychology pair, the MS paper had effort as its outcome, while the other centred on beliefs; the board split 50/50.
- **EDITED**: A model or analysis that explains empirical patterns and draws out unexpected implications for value creation or policy.
  - *now*: A model guided by causal or descriptive empirical results that explains the patterns and draws unexpected implications for value creation, welfare, or policy.
  - *why*: An MS winner first got causal results from a sudden event and then built a model guided by them, beating a paper that only cited game theory to motivate a hypothesis.
- **EDITED**: Coining a new concept, paradox, or named hypothesis as the headline contribution rather than a model result or a quantified finding.
  - *now*: Headline is a newly coined construct, belief, or paradox whose effects are shown mainly through moderation in survey or vignette studies, not behavioural data.
  - *why*: An MS paper with a coined term won on large-scale behavioural data, while a coined belief construct tested by moderation across many studies lost; the flag needed to be narrower.
- **EDITED**: Abstracts mostly made of 'we argue/theorize/hypothesize' statements with no results or magnitudes reported.
  - *now*: Abstract reports only argued, theorized, postulated, or simulated implications ('we argue', 'the model suggests') without empirical tests or analytic results.
  - *why*: Merged with the post-hoc-propositions flag and extended to simulation-only implications, which lost to an RCT.
- **DROPPED**: Theorizing propositions after the fact from observational patterns ('we propose a self-limiting nature') instead of testing or modeling them.
  - *why*: Merged into the argued/theorized/simulated-implications flag to free a slot.
- **EDITED**: Framed mainly as extending a named management theory or literature, with neither a formal model nor a causal design to anchor it.
  - *now*: Framed as extending a named management theory, or invoking a theory only to motivate a hypothesis, with neither a formal model nor causal design.
  - *why*: A paper that cited game theory but tested a strategy construct correlationally was wrongly favoured over a causal-plus-model paper.
- **EDITED**: Main focus on subjective experience (identity, status, felt fit, stress, wellbeing) as the outcome or headline mechanism rather than behaviour or incentives.
  - *now*: Main outcome is subjective experience or beliefs (identity, status felt, fit, wellbeing, perceived value); perceptions as mediators of behaviour are acceptable.
  - *why*: The MS status paper used perceived expectations as a mediator of effort and was wrongly penalized; the flag should target outcomes, not mediators.
- **ADDED**: Contribution framed as resolving an organizational tension or trade-off (novelty vs. value, tradition vs. innovation, collective vs. individual) rather than an economic mechanism.
  - *why*: Several non-MS papers, including a field experiment, framed themselves around org-theory tensions or conundrums.
- **EDITED**: Correlational panel results ('is associated with') stacked with moderators or moderated mediation, with no exogenous variation addressing endogeneity.
  - *now*: Correlational results ('is associated with', 'suggestive evidence') stacked with moderators or moderated mediation, with no exogenous variation addressing endogeneity.
  - *why*: A losing startup-hiring paper relied on associations and 'suggestive evidence' for its mechanisms.
