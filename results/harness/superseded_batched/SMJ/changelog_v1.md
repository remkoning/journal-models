# Changelog v1 (revision of v0)

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 1: val accuracy 97.5% (vs OrgSci 95.0%, vs MgmtSci 100.0%); best so far 97.5%, threshold 96.5%. Revision of v0.

Train pool: v0 judged 39 train pairs, accuracy 98.7% (vs OrgSci 97.4%, vs MgmtSci 100.0%). Reflection saw 1 misjudged, 8 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **ADDED**: The firm, or the founder acting for the venture, is the decision-maker, and the abstract studies a strategic choice such as entry, positioning, investment, scope or innovation direction.
  - *why*: The SMJ articles in these pairs consistently frame the question as a strategic decision, such as industry choice or entry into nascent versus established markets.
- **ADDED**: The outcomes are at firm level: performance, value creation, growth-option value, revenue or competitive advantage, not only individual or audience outcomes.
  - *why*: SMJ winners linked their findings to firm value or performance, while counterparts stopped at employee exit or at theoretical mechanisms.
- **ADDED**: Effects depend on contingencies: the abstract shows how strategy–environment fit or firm and industry characteristics strengthen or weaken the main relationship.
  - *why*: SMJ abstracts emphasized alignment and moderation, for example investment strategy matched to uncertainty, or effects varying by firm and industry traits.
- **ADDED**: The abstract names the costs, trade-offs or unintended penalties of strategic moves for the firm, such as change, repositioning, path dependence or departing from tradition.
  - *why*: The misjudged identity-ambiguity pair and the tradition-renewal pairs both framed audience effects as costs a firm bears for strategic change.
- **ADDED**: Individual or employee-level mechanisms (micro-foundations) are explicitly tied to firm-level results, such as leaders affecting performance or worker interventions raising revenue.
  - *why*: The field experiment that reported revenue effects beat the study limited to employee turnover; the female-leadership anchor also links micro to macro.
- **ADDED**: The study covers strategic leadership and governance: top teams, dominant coalitions, family control or CEO succession, and their link to firm strategy or performance feedback.
  - *why*: In the dominant-coalition pair, the empirical study of CEO succession and performance feedback beat the conceptual piece on politics and adaptation.
- **ADDED**: Hypotheses are derived from core strategy theories (resource-based, real options, behavioral theory of the firm, capabilities, competitive dynamics) and tested on archival or field-experimental data.
  - *why*: SMJ winners were deductive tests of strategy theories, whereas the losers were cases, models or conceptual pieces.
- **ADDED**: The abstract explicitly claims contributions to strategy literatures, such as competitive, corporate, complementor, entrepreneurial or international strategy, and states what managers should do.
  - *why*: The anchors and winners explicitly informed firm strategy, for example complementor strategy.
- **ADDED**: Customer or audience reactions are framed as determinants of the value or cost of a firm's strategic choice, not as phenomena of evaluator cognition in their own right.
  - *why*: This separates the SMJ audience-reaction papers from the categorization papers centered on evaluators.
- **ADDED**: A formal or analytical model deriving optimal design, pricing or fee policies without empirical testing suggests an operations, economics or marketing journal.
  - *why*: In the platform anchor, the analytical model of optimal design appeared elsewhere.
- **ADDED**: An economics or finance framing: testing information asymmetry with market-price predictions, macro or local economic outcomes, or impacts at the level of public policy.
  - *why*: The franchising information paper and the university-science paper had an economics or policy framing and appeared outside SMJ.
- **ADDED**: Personnel or labor outcomes such as exit, promotion, aspirations or gender gaps, studied without any link to firm strategy or firm performance.
  - *why*: The ranking-curve exit study and the lawyer-aspirations study lacked any link to firm strategy.
- **ADDED**: A sociological focus on categories, identity or evaluator perception where the producer is an individual and the firm's strategic value is absent.
  - *why*: The categorical-stickiness paper about individual authors lost to the firm-value framing of tradition renewal.
- **ADDED**: An inductive comparative case study that builds a process theory from a handful of firms suggests a qualitative organization-theory outlet.
  - *why*: The multi-case process theory of venture growth appeared elsewhere, while the deductive study of founder entry choices was the SMJ article.
- **ADDED**: Purely conceptual or simulation arguments about organizational adaptation, or about reconciling theoretical paradigms, with no empirical test of strategic outcomes.
  - *why*: The conceptual piece on organizational politics and adaptation lost to an empirical study of political slack.
- **ADDED**: The main contribution is settling a debate between theories of the firm, rather than explaining a strategic choice and what follows from it.
  - *why*: The paper reconciling the opportunism debate lost to a paper on how multinational investment creates value.
