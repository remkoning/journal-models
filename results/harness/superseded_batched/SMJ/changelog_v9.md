# Changelog v9 (revision of v8)

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 9: val accuracy 97.5% (vs OrgSci 95.0%, vs MgmtSci 100.0%); best so far 97.5%, threshold 96.5%. Revision of v8.

Train pool: v8 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 97.5% (vs OrgSci 95.0%, vs MgmtSci 100.0%). Reflection saw 1 misjudged, 8 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Individual-level mechanisms are tied to firm-level strategic results such as performance, search, exploration or knowledge creation, not to HR outcomes like retention.
  - *now*: Individual outcomes count when tied to firm strategy: individual mechanisms that drive firm search or performance, or employee mobility and spawning caused by firm strategic actions.
  - *why*: An SMJ paper studied employee entrepreneurship as a consequence of acquisitions; the old rule pushed against it.
- **EDITED**: The study covers strategic leadership and governance: top teams, dominant coalitions, family control or CEO succession, and their link to firm strategy or performance feedback.
  - *now*: Strategic leadership and governance: boards, top teams, family control, CEO or director succession and turnover, explained through behavioral, legitimacy or power dynamics inside the firm.
  - *why*: The board misjudged an SMJ behavioral-governance paper on director turnover and favored an event-study counterpart.
- **EDITED**: Hypotheses come from core strategy theories and are tested on archival data, field experiments, lab or vignette experiments with decision-makers, or formal models plus empirical tests.
  - *now*: Hypotheses come from strategy theories, tested on archival data or experiments; formal models or simulations qualify when they prescribe how firms should make strategic choices.
  - *why*: SMJ publishes prescriptive simulations and formal resource models without an empirical test; the old rule underweighted them.
- **EDITED**: Behavioral strategy: managerial cognition, biases, attention or monitoring explain firm strategic behavior such as risk taking, search or competitive response.
  - *now*: Behavioral strategy and upper echelons: executive personality, cognition, biases or attention explain firm strategic behavior such as risk taking, CSR, search or competitive response.
  - *why*: The SMJ paper linking CEO traits to CSR was underrated against a paper on media evaluations.
- **ADDED**: Firms' proactive or comparative-cost responses to threats or partner changes, such as make-versus-buy shifts or preemptive moves, framed as deliberate strategic choices.
  - *why*: SMJ papers on sourcing-mode shifts and preemptive responses beat counterparts framed around context or governance conditions, but only with low confidence.
- **EDITED**: An economics or finance framing: attributing firm outcomes to exogenous shocks, market-price or analyst predictability, industrial-organization deterrence, or macro and policy outcomes rather than managerial choice.
  - *now*: Economics or finance framing: capital-market pressures, investor stock-price reactions, exogenous shocks or economy-wide aggregate trends drive outcomes rather than managerial choice.
  - *why*: The non-SMJ counterparts relied on investor reactions or capital-market pressure and drew economy-wide implications.
- **EDITED**: Personnel outcomes such as turnover, exit, promotion, aspirations or gender gaps, studied as HR outcomes even when called firm-relevant, without a strategy or performance link.
  - *now*: Rank-and-file HR outcomes such as turnover, promotion, hiring callbacks or gender gaps, studied as labor-market phenomena not caused by firm strategy nor affecting performance.
  - *why*: The old rule wrongly flagged director turnover and employee entrepreneurship caused by acquisitions, both of which SMJ published.
- **EDITED**: A sociological focus on categories, identity or evaluator perception where the producer is an individual and the firm's strategic value is absent.
  - *now*: A sociological or infomediary focus where the dependent variable is evaluators' judgment itself, such as media, critic or recruiter perceptions, rather than firm strategic value.
  - *why*: Non-SMJ papers centered on media disapproval or recruiter evaluation as the outcome itself.
- **ADDED**: Regional, cultural or institutional context is the main explanatory variable and firm choice is incidental, framed as a contextual or macro determinant study.
  - *why*: A non-SMJ paper explained sourcing by regional trust rather than by the firm's own comparative-cost choice.
