## What Strategic Management Journal values
1. The firm, or the founder acting for the venture, is the decision-maker, and the abstract studies a strategic choice such as entry, positioning, investment, scope or innovation direction.
2. Outcomes are at firm level: performance, innovation, growth, revenue, competitive advantage, or investors' valuation of the firm (e.g., abnormal returns), not only outcomes for individuals or audiences.
3. Effects depend on contingencies: firm resources, competitive intensity, strategy–environment fit or firm ties strengthen or weaken the main relationship.
4. The abstract names the costs, trade-offs or unintended penalties of strategic moves for the firm, such as change, repositioning, path dependence or departing from tradition.
5. Micro-foundations link levels in either direction: employee or leader mechanisms drive firm results, or firm strategy and growth shape employee outcomes.
6. Strategic leadership and governance: top teams, CEO traits such as founder status or overconfidence, family control or succession in established firms, even without a performance outcome.
7. Theory comes from strategy frameworks (resource-based, real options, Penrosean growth, strategic human capital, stakeholder, upper echelons, competitive dynamics), whatever the empirical method.
8. The abstract explicitly claims contributions to strategy literatures (competitive, corporate, nonmarket, entrepreneurial, international, human capital) or to competitive advantage, and says what managers should do.
9. Customer, investor or audience reactions are framed as determinants of the value or cost of a firm's strategic choice, not as evaluator cognition in their own right.
10. Nonmarket strategy: political connections, lobbying, stakeholder or activist relations treated as firm resources or strategies whose value depends on firm actions.
11. Lab or field experiments manipulate an organizational or managerial lever (monitoring, incentives, structure, communication) and theorize its effect on strategic behavior such as search.

## What Strategic Management Journal does not value / red flags
1. A formal or analytical model deriving optimal design, pricing or fee policies without empirical testing suggests an operations, economics or marketing journal.
2. Economics or political-science framing: regulatory capture, self-enforcing contract theory, macro or public-policy impacts, with no firm strategy explaining variation in outcomes.
3. Personnel or labor outcomes such as exit, promotion, aspirations or gender gaps, studied without any link to firm strategy, firm growth or firm performance.
4. The main contribution goes to sociological literatures (social movements, categories, status, identity) rather than strategy, even when firms are the units studied.
5. An inductive case study building a process theory for organization theory, with no link to competitive advantage or strategic outcomes, suggests a qualitative organization-theory outlet.
6. Purely conceptual or simulation arguments, or settling a debate between theories of the firm, with no empirical test of a strategic choice and its consequences.
7. Studies of individual aspiring entrepreneurs, their pitches, training or investor funding decisions, rather than firm or executive strategy, suggest an entrepreneurship journal.
8. Explanations centred on country institutions or foreign-versus-domestic firm differences, rather than firm resources or strategies, suggest an international business journal.
