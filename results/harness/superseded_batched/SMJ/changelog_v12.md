# Changelog v12 (revision of v10)

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 12: val accuracy 97.5% (vs OrgSci 95.0%, vs MgmtSci 100.0%); best so far 97.5%, threshold 96.5%. Revision of v10.

Train pool: v10 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 96.2% (vs OrgSci 97.5%, vs MgmtSci 95.0%). Reflection saw 2 misjudged, 7 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: The firm, or the founder acting for the venture, is the decision-maker, studying a strategic choice: entry, positioning, investment, scope, sourcing, preemptive response or innovation direction.
  - *now*: The firm, or the founder acting for the venture, is the decision-maker, studying a strategic choice: entry, positioning, investment, scope, sourcing, alliance partners, preemptive response or innovation direction.
  - *why*: Alliance partner-addition choices driven by firm network position were underweighted versus bargaining over contract terms.
- **EDITED**: Individual-level outcomes count when explained by firm-level strategic conditions (growth, strategy, resource frictions) through a named strategy theory such as Penrosean or resource-based theory.
  - *now*: Microfoundations: individuals' search, knowledge seeking, collaboration, or persist-versus-exit decisions, explained by incentives, hierarchy or cost–benefit trade-offs, even in lab experiments.
  - *why*: SMJ picked micro studies of search under monitoring, knowledge seeking costs, and choosing to collaborate; the board treated them as non-strategy individual work.
- **EDITED**: Behavioral strategy and upper echelons: traits, biases or experience of executives, including those below the CEO, characterize or explain firm decisions and capabilities.
  - *now*: Behavioral strategy and upper echelons: traits, emotions, biases or experience of executives, founders or entrepreneurial teams explain firm decisions, strategic distinctiveness or capabilities.
  - *why*: Team emotions driving escalation-versus-termination and CEO traits driving strategic deviation were SMJ but judged only weakly.
- **EDITED**: Nonmarket strategy: political ties, regulation or crises treated as firm resources or risks whose gain or loss changes firm innovation, performance or business-model viability.
  - *now*: Nonmarket strategy: political ties, lobbying or crises treated as firm-specific resources or risks changing innovation or performance; aggregate regulatory intensity alone is weaker evidence.
  - *why*: An aggregate regulation-and-innovation paper was wrongly favored because of the broad nonmarket rule.
- **EDITED**: Labor-market or HR-practice studies (hiring, training, turnover, gender gaps) judged as practice effectiveness, not tied to strategy theory or firm strategic choice.
  - *now*: HR or employee-welfare interventions (onboarding, volunteering, training, hiring, gender gaps) judged by turnover, attitudes or fairness, not tied to strategy theory or firm choice.
  - *why*: A field experiment on an employee prosocial program reducing turnover was mistaken for SMJ despite being an HR-effectiveness study.
- **EDITED**: Organization-theory core (institutions, logics, status, categories, population-level learning) as the main contribution, even with a firm-action outcome, unless explicitly tied to competitive advantage.
  - *now*: Organization-theory core (institutions, logics, status, categories, collective action, organizational climate) as the main contribution, even with a firm-action outcome, unless tied to competitive advantage.
  - *why*: Collective-action and organizational-climate framings appeared in non-SMJ counterparts of micro team and knowledge studies.
- **EDITED**: Formal mathematical work that defines constructs or derives optimal policies, with no empirical test and no synthesis of applications, suggests an economics or operations outlet.
  - *now*: Formal models, simulations or conceptual arguments that define constructs or derive optimal policies, with no empirical test, no synthesis, and no firm strategic choice.
  - *why*: Merged two overlapping rules on unempirical formal and simulation work to free a slot; confident anchors still hold.
- **DROPPED**: Simulation or conceptual arguments about organizational adaptation or reconciling paradigms that name no strategic choice actors make and test no strategic outcome.
  - *why*: Folded into the merged formal-model red flag.
- **ADDED**: International-business lens as core theory (home, host and third-country contexts, subsidiary embeddedness) suggests an international business outlet over a general strategy one.
  - *why*: A paper on knowledge networks across multinational home, host and third countries was wrongly favored over a domain-general absorptive-capacity study.
