# Strategic Management Journal rulebook v9

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 9: val accuracy 97.5% (vs OrgSci 95.0%, vs MgmtSci 100.0%); best so far 97.5%, threshold 96.5%. Revision of v8.

## What Strategic Management Journal values
1. The firm, or the founder acting for the venture, is the decision-maker, and the abstract studies a strategic choice such as entry, positioning, investment, scope or innovation direction.
2. The outcomes are at firm level: performance, value creation, growth-option value, revenue or competitive advantage, not only individual or audience outcomes.
3. Effects depend on contingencies: the abstract shows how strategy–environment fit or firm and industry characteristics strengthen or weaken the main relationship.
4. The abstract names the costs, trade-offs or unintended penalties of strategic moves for the firm, such as change, repositioning, path dependence or departing from tradition.
5. Individual outcomes count when tied to firm strategy: individual mechanisms that drive firm search or performance, or employee mobility and spawning caused by firm strategic actions.
6. Strategic leadership and governance: boards, top teams, family control, CEO or director succession and turnover, explained through behavioral, legitimacy or power dynamics inside the firm.
7. Hypotheses come from strategy theories, tested on archival data or experiments; formal models or simulations qualify when they prescribe how firms should make strategic choices.
8. The abstract explicitly claims contributions to strategy literatures, such as competitive, corporate, complementor, entrepreneurial or international strategy, and states what managers should do.
9. Customer or audience reactions are framed as determinants of the value or cost of a firm's strategic choice, not as phenomena of evaluator cognition in their own right.
10. Behavioral strategy and upper echelons: executive personality, cognition, biases or attention explain firm strategic behavior such as risk taking, CSR, search or competitive response.
11. Reviews or conceptual typologies are acceptable when they synthesize a strategy theory stream or theorize strategies actors use to win resource-holders' support.
12. Firms' proactive or comparative-cost responses to threats or partner changes, such as make-versus-buy shifts or preemptive moves, framed as deliberate strategic choices.

## What Strategic Management Journal does not value / red flags
1. Formal mathematical work that defines constructs or derives optimal policies, with no empirical test and no synthesis of applications, suggests an economics or operations outlet.
2. Economics or finance framing: capital-market pressures, investor stock-price reactions, exogenous shocks or economy-wide aggregate trends drive outcomes rather than managerial choice.
3. Rank-and-file HR outcomes such as turnover, promotion, hiring callbacks or gender gaps, studied as labor-market phenomena not caused by firm strategy nor affecting performance.
4. A sociological or infomediary focus where the dependent variable is evaluators' judgment itself, such as media, critic or recruiter perceptions, rather than firm strategic value.
5. Inductive or historical case studies contributing mainly to organization theory (institutions, logics, organizing) rather than to strategy process, practice or competitive outcomes.
6. Simulation or conceptual arguments about organizational adaptation or reconciling paradigms that name no strategic choice actors make and test no strategic outcome.
7. The main contribution is settling a debate between theories of the firm, rather than explaining a strategic choice and what follows from it.
8. Regional, cultural or institutional context is the main explanatory variable and firm choice is incidental, framed as a contextual or macro determinant study.
