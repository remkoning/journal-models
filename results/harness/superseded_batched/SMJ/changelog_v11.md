# Changelog v11 (revision of v10)

**REJECTED** at iteration 11: val accuracy 95.0% (vs OrgSci 95.0%, vs MgmtSci 95.0%); best so far 97.5%, threshold 96.5%. Revision of v10.

Train pool: v10 judged 39 train pairs, accuracy 97.4% (vs OrgSci 94.7%, vs MgmtSci 100.0%). Reflection saw 1 misjudged, 8 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: The outcomes are at firm level: performance, value creation, growth-option value, revenue or competitive advantage, not only individual or audience outcomes.
  - *now*: Outcomes are firm-level (performance, value creation, growth, survival, competitive advantage); individual outcomes count only when explained by firm strategy conditions through a named strategy theory.
  - *why*: Merged with the old individual-outcomes rule to free a slot; the venture-profitability vs. career-choice pair shows the firm-level outcome is the deciding cue.
- **DROPPED**: Individual-level outcomes count when explained by firm-level strategic conditions (growth, strategy, resource frictions) through a named strategy theory such as Penrosean or resource-based theory.
  - *why*: Folded into the firm-level outcomes rule.
- **ADDED**: Multi-unit or multinational firms: how the parent's internal configuration of units or inventor networks shapes knowledge transfer, productivity or innovation inside the firm.
  - *why*: SMJ articles on internal clustering of establishments and MNC inventor networks are corporate-strategy questions that beat studies of external inputs or individual inventors.
- **EDITED**: Hypotheses come from strategy theories, tested on archival data or experiments; formal models or simulations qualify when they prescribe how firms should make strategic choices.
  - *now*: Hypotheses come from strategy theories, tested on archival data or experiments; formal models qualify when they derive core strategy phenomena such as risk–return, rivalry or performance heterogeneity.
  - *why*: The board under-rated a formal model that derives risk–return relations from competitive interaction, a classic strategy puzzle.
- **EDITED**: Customer or audience reactions are framed as determinants of the value or cost of a firm's strategic choice, not as phenomena of evaluator cognition in their own right.
  - *now*: Customer, analyst or other audience reactions count when framed as the value or cost of a firm's strategic choice or change, not as evaluator cognition itself.
  - *why*: An SMJ paper treating analyst churn and inaccuracy as an external cost of repositioning was misjudged; analysts need to be explicitly covered.
- **EDITED**: Nonmarket strategy: political ties, regulation or crises treated as firm resources or risks whose gain or loss changes firm innovation, performance or business-model viability.
  - *now*: Nonmarket strategy: political ties, regulation, policy or crises treated as firm resources or risks whose gain or loss changes firm innovation, performance or business-model viability.
  - *why*: Policy shocks were judged correctly in SMJ only when they altered venture or firm performance; adding 'policy' makes this rule apply to such cases.
- **EDITED**: Formal mathematical work that defines constructs or derives optimal policies, with no empirical test and no synthesis of applications, suggests an economics or operations outlet.
  - *now*: Formal mathematical work that derives optimal contracts or operational policies, with no empirical test and no link to a core strategy phenomenon, suggests an economics or operations outlet.
  - *why*: The old wording penalized SMJ formal theory on risk–return; the narrower wording still catches optimal-contract models.
- **EDITED**: Labor-market or HR-practice studies (hiring, training, turnover, gender gaps) judged as practice effectiveness, not tied to strategy theory or firm strategic choice.
  - *now*: Labor-market or HR-practice studies (hiring, training, mobility, turnover, gender gaps) judged as practice effectiveness or individual careers, not tied to firm strategic choice or firm outcomes.
  - *why*: Employee mobility and career-choice studies were the non-SMJ side in several pairs.
- **EDITED**: Evaluator focus: the dependent variable is investors', critics', media's or recruiters' judgments of individuals or offerings, rather than firm strategic value.
  - *now*: Evaluator focus: the dependent variable is investors', critics', media's or recruiters' judgments as an evaluation phenomenon, unconnected to the cost or value of a firm's strategic move.
  - *why*: This flag wrongly counted against an SMJ paper where audience accuracy measured the cost of repositioning.
- **EDITED**: Organization-theory core (institutions, logics, status, categories, population-level learning) as the main contribution, even with a firm-action outcome, unless explicitly tied to competitive advantage.
  - *now*: Organization-theory core (institutions, logics, status, categories, authenticity, population-level learning) as the main contribution, unless explicitly tied to a firm's strategic choice and its costs or advantage.
  - *why*: Identity concepts framed as costs of a strategic move belong in SMJ; authenticity-paradox framing does not.
- **EDITED**: Simulation or conceptual arguments about organizational adaptation or reconciling paradigms that name no strategic choice actors make and test no strategic outcome.
  - *now*: Simulation or conceptual work on organizational adaptation, or settling debates between theories of the firm, that names no strategic choice and tests no strategic outcome.
  - *why*: Merged two related flags to free a slot for a new pattern.
- **DROPPED**: The main contribution is settling a debate between theories of the firm, rather than explaining a strategic choice and what follows from it.
  - *why*: Folded into the merged simulation/conceptual flag.
- **ADDED**: Decisions modeled mainly as learning from external information signals (customer feedback, rival failures) under uncertainty, without firm performance consequences, suggest a management-science or organization-science outlet.
  - *why*: In two pairs the non-SMJ paper framed firm choices as information-driven learning, while the SMJ paper stressed costs or performance.
- **EDITED**: Institutional or development context (voids, political systems, inclusion) is the main explanatory variable or societal welfare the outcome, with firm choice incidental.
  - *now*: Institutional context, public policy or public goods (voids, immigration rules, public data) is the main explanation, with individual careers, industry-wide shares or welfare as outcome and firm choice incidental.
  - *why*: The non-SMJ papers tied external public inputs to industry-wide entrant shares or individual careers rather than to firm strategy.
