## What Strategic Management Journal values
1. The firm, or the founder acting for the venture, is the decision-maker, and the abstract studies a strategic choice such as entry, positioning, investment, scope or innovation direction.
2. The outcomes are at firm level: performance, value creation, growth-option value, revenue or competitive advantage, not only individual or audience outcomes.
3. Effects depend on contingencies: the abstract shows how strategy–environment fit or firm and industry characteristics strengthen or weaken the main relationship.
4. The abstract names the costs, trade-offs or unintended penalties of strategic moves for the firm, such as change, repositioning, path dependence or departing from tradition.
5. Individual or employee-level mechanisms (micro-foundations) are explicitly tied to firm-level results, such as leaders affecting performance or worker interventions raising revenue.
6. The study covers strategic leadership and governance, such as top teams, boards, family control or CEO succession, and links it to firm strategy or performance.
7. Arguments draw on strategy-native theories (resource-based, knowledge-based, real options, capabilities, competitive dynamics, stakeholder strategy) rather than generic organization or psychology theories.
8. The abstract explicitly claims contributions to strategy literatures, such as competitive, corporate, complementor, entrepreneurial or international strategy, and states what managers should do.
9. Audience reactions, or entrepreneurs' readings of market meaning, are framed as shaping a firm's strategic choice or its value, not studied as evaluator cognition for its own sake.
10. Conceptual theory-building, reviews or formal propositions about core strategy constructs, such as capabilities, competitive advantage, real options or rivalry-cooperation, fit the journal even without data.
11. How the firm configures itself (unit location, scope, structure, or how alliances are spread across units) explains differences in productivity or performance across firms.
12. Human capital, networks and stakeholder relationships are treated as resources the firm or founder assembles and manages, with effects on growth, innovation or value.

## What Strategic Management Journal does not value / red flags
1. A formal or analytical model deriving optimal design, pricing or fee policies without empirical testing suggests an operations, economics or marketing journal.
2. An economics or finance framing: investor returns, information asymmetry tested with market prices, effects of public programs, or outcomes at the policy or regional level.
3. Personnel or labor outcomes such as exit, promotion, aspirations or gender gaps, studied without linking them to how firms assemble resources or to firm performance.
4. A sociological focus on categories, labels, identity or evaluator perception, where the unit is a word, product or individual and no firm strategic choice is involved.
5. Inductive qualitative work that builds organizational process theory but makes no claim about competitive advantage or strategy suggests an organization-theory outlet.
6. Computational or simulation models of individual decision biases or of organizational adaptation, with no empirical test of firm strategic outcomes.
7. The main contribution is settling a debate between theories of the firm or of decision-making, rather than explaining a strategic choice and what follows from it.
8. Stylized laboratory tasks that induce or measure a construct, contributing mainly to learning, transfer or psychology theory rather than to firm strategy.
