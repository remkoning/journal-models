# Changelog v5 (revision of v1)

**REJECTED** at iteration 5: val accuracy 95.0% (vs OrgSci 95.0%, vs MgmtSci 95.0%); best so far 97.5%, threshold 96.5%. Revision of v1.

Train pool: v1 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 97.5% (vs OrgSci 95.0%, vs MgmtSci 100.0%). Reflection saw 1 misjudged, 8 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Individual or employee-level mechanisms (micro-foundations) are explicitly tied to firm-level results, such as leaders affecting performance or worker interventions raising revenue.
  - *now*: Micro-foundations link levels either way: individuals shape firm results, or firm actions such as acquisitions or relocations reshape employees' careers, mobility or entrepreneurship.
  - *why*: SMJ published a study of employee outcomes caused by a firm's strategic action, and the board wrongly ranked it below a firm-performance paper.
- **EDITED**: The study covers strategic leadership and governance: top teams, dominant coalitions, family control or CEO succession, and their link to firm strategy or performance feedback.
  - *now*: The study covers strategic leadership and governance: boards, top teams, family control, CEO succession or dismissal, and what these mean for firm strategy or value.
  - *why*: The misjudged pair: SMJ took a study of boards declining to dismiss executives and the market consequences of doing so.
- **EDITED**: Hypotheses are derived from core strategy theories (resource-based, real options, behavioral theory of the firm, capabilities, competitive dynamics) and tested on archival or field-experimental data.
  - *now*: Hypotheses come from core strategy theories (resource-based, real options, behavioral theory, capabilities, competitive dynamics) and are tested on archival or field-experimental data.
  - *why*: Shortened to fit the length limit and to sit alongside the new simulation rule; meaning unchanged.
- **ADDED**: Computational models such as NK simulations are fine when they give prescriptions for how firms should organize search, innovation or structure.
  - *why*: SMJ published a simulation that told firms how to allocate inventors, and the old simulation red flag pushed the board the wrong way.
- **ADDED**: Nonmarket strategy: political connections, CSR or stakeholder engagement are treated as firm strategies, with their sources, differences between firms and payoffs explained.
  - *why*: The SMJ papers on corporate political influence and on firms' CSR strategies won only with low confidence.
- **ADDED**: Qualitative process studies are fine when they explain how firms or alliances tackle strategic problems and how strategy-making unfolds over time.
  - *why*: SMJ published longitudinal case studies on joint sensemaking about strategic problems, and the old case-study red flag worked against them.
- **EDITED**: An economics or finance framing: testing information asymmetry with market-price predictions, macro or local economic outcomes, or impacts at the level of public policy.
  - *now*: Economics or finance framing: capital-market or investor-portfolio motives, price-based information tests, economy-wide trends, consumer welfare or antitrust as the main stakes.
  - *why*: The non-SMJ papers centred on capital-market pressure, common owners' portfolio motives, or user welfare and antitrust at market level.
- **EDITED**: Personnel or labor outcomes such as exit, promotion, aspirations or gender gaps, studied without any link to firm strategy or firm performance.
  - *now*: Rank-and-file personnel outcomes such as aspirations, promotion or gender gaps, studied with no link to firm actions, strategy or performance.
  - *why*: Executive exit decided by boards is governance and employee outcomes caused by acquisitions are strategy, so 'exit' wrongly penalized SMJ papers.
- **EDITED**: An inductive comparative case study that builds a process theory from a handful of firms suggests a qualitative organization-theory outlet.
  - *now*: Inductive studies of meaning-making, change resistance or ambiguity, set outside firms and adding to organization theory rather than strategy process, suggest another outlet.
  - *why*: Both papers in the qualitative pair were case studies; what separated them was the setting and which literature they spoke to, not the method.
- **EDITED**: Purely conceptual or simulation arguments about organizational adaptation, or about reconciling theoretical paradigms, with no empirical test of strategic outcomes.
  - *now*: Purely conceptual work, or work mainly settling debates between theories of the firm, with no empirical test or firm-level prescription.
  - *why*: Simulations giving firm prescriptions appear in SMJ; merged with the old theory-debate flag to free a slot.
- **DROPPED**: The main contribution is settling a debate between theories of the firm, rather than explaining a strategic choice and what follows from it.
  - *why*: Merged into the edited conceptual-work red flag.
- **ADDED**: The thing explained is executives' personal motives, ideology or self-expression rather than the firm's strategic choices and what follows from them.
  - *why*: The non-SMJ political paper explained executives' personal expressive versus strategic donation motives, not firm outcomes.
