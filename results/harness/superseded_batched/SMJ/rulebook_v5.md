## What Strategic Management Journal values
1. The firm, or the founder acting for the venture, is the decision-maker, and the abstract studies a strategic choice such as entry, positioning, investment, scope or innovation direction.
2. The outcomes are at firm level: performance, value creation, growth-option value, revenue or competitive advantage, not only individual or audience outcomes.
3. Effects depend on contingencies: the abstract shows how strategy–environment fit or firm and industry characteristics strengthen or weaken the main relationship.
4. The abstract names the costs, trade-offs or unintended penalties of strategic moves for the firm, such as change, repositioning, path dependence or departing from tradition.
5. Micro-foundations link levels either way: individuals shape firm results, or firm actions such as acquisitions or relocations reshape employees' careers, mobility or entrepreneurship.
6. The study covers strategic leadership and governance: boards, top teams, family control, CEO succession or dismissal, and what these mean for firm strategy or value.
7. Hypotheses come from core strategy theories (resource-based, real options, behavioral theory, capabilities, competitive dynamics) and are tested on archival or field-experimental data.
8. Computational models such as NK simulations are fine when they give prescriptions for how firms should organize search, innovation or structure.
9. The abstract explicitly claims contributions to strategy literatures, such as competitive, corporate, complementor, entrepreneurial or international strategy, and states what managers should do.
10. Customer or audience reactions are framed as determinants of the value or cost of a firm's strategic choice, not as phenomena of evaluator cognition in their own right.
11. Nonmarket strategy: political connections, CSR or stakeholder engagement are treated as firm strategies, with their sources, differences between firms and payoffs explained.
12. Qualitative process studies are fine when they explain how firms or alliances tackle strategic problems and how strategy-making unfolds over time.

## What Strategic Management Journal does not value / red flags
1. A formal or analytical model deriving optimal design, pricing or fee policies without empirical testing suggests an operations, economics or marketing journal.
2. Economics or finance framing: capital-market or investor-portfolio motives, price-based information tests, economy-wide trends, consumer welfare or antitrust as the main stakes.
3. Rank-and-file personnel outcomes such as aspirations, promotion or gender gaps, studied with no link to firm actions, strategy or performance.
4. A sociological focus on categories, identity or evaluator perception where the producer is an individual and the firm's strategic value is absent.
5. Inductive studies of meaning-making, change resistance or ambiguity, set outside firms and adding to organization theory rather than strategy process, suggest another outlet.
6. Purely conceptual work, or work mainly settling debates between theories of the firm, with no empirical test or firm-level prescription.
7. The thing explained is executives' personal motives, ideology or self-expression rather than the firm's strategic choices and what follows from them.
