# Changelog v8 (revision of v1)

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 8: val accuracy 97.5% (vs OrgSci 95.0%, vs MgmtSci 100.0%); best so far 97.5%, threshold 96.5%. Revision of v1.

Train pool: v1 judged 39 train pairs, accuracy 89.7% (vs OrgSci 89.5%, vs MgmtSci 90.0%). Reflection saw 4 misjudged, 5 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Individual or employee-level mechanisms (micro-foundations) are explicitly tied to firm-level results, such as leaders affecting performance or worker interventions raising revenue.
  - *now*: Individual-level mechanisms are tied to firm-level strategic results such as performance, search, exploration or knowledge creation, not to HR outcomes like retention.
  - *why*: An employee intervention with a retention outcome was wrongly favored over a lab study of managerial influence on strategic search.
- **EDITED**: Hypotheses are derived from core strategy theories (resource-based, real options, behavioral theory of the firm, capabilities, competitive dynamics) and tested on archival or field-experimental data.
  - *now*: Hypotheses come from core strategy theories and are tested on archival data, field experiments, lab or vignette experiments with decision-makers, or formal models plus empirical tests.
  - *why*: SMJ articles using lab or manager experiments and formal model plus empirical test were under-credited.
- **ADDED**: Behavioral strategy: managerial cognition, biases, attention or monitoring explain firm strategic behavior such as risk taking, search or competitive response.
  - *why*: SMJ winners explained risk taking, search and competitive response through managerial cognition, while rivals used external or economic explanations.
- **ADDED**: Reviews or conceptual typologies are acceptable when they synthesize a strategy theory stream or theorize strategies actors use to win resource-holders' support.
  - *why*: The board missed SMJ's conceptual or review pieces because it treated any non-empirical work as a red flag.
- **EDITED**: A formal or analytical model deriving optimal design, pricing or fee policies without empirical testing suggests an operations, economics or marketing journal.
  - *now*: Formal mathematical work that defines constructs or derives optimal policies, with no empirical test and no synthesis of applications, suggests an economics or operations outlet.
  - *why*: Pure formalization of a strategy construct appeared elsewhere, while a review of the same theory stream appeared in SMJ.
- **EDITED**: An economics or finance framing: testing information asymmetry with market-price predictions, macro or local economic outcomes, or impacts at the level of public policy.
  - *now*: An economics or finance framing: attributing firm outcomes to exogenous shocks, market-price or analyst predictability, industrial-organization deterrence, or macro and policy outcomes rather than managerial choice.
  - *why*: Shock-vulnerability and IO entry-deterrence abstracts lost to SMJ abstracts that explained the outcome through managerial choice.
- **EDITED**: Personnel or labor outcomes such as exit, promotion, aspirations or gender gaps, studied without any link to firm strategy or firm performance.
  - *now*: Personnel outcomes such as turnover, exit, promotion, aspirations or gender gaps, studied as HR outcomes even when called firm-relevant, without a strategy or performance link.
  - *why*: A turnover study claimed relevance to the firm and fooled the board.
- **EDITED**: An inductive comparative case study that builds a process theory from a handful of firms suggests a qualitative organization-theory outlet.
  - *now*: Inductive or historical case studies contributing mainly to organization theory (institutions, logics, organizing) rather than to strategy process, practice or competitive outcomes.
  - *why*: SMJ does publish qualitative strategy-practice and historical decision studies, so the flag should target the organization-theory contribution, not the method.
- **EDITED**: Purely conceptual or simulation arguments about organizational adaptation, or about reconciling theoretical paradigms, with no empirical test of strategic outcomes.
  - *now*: Simulation or conceptual arguments about organizational adaptation or reconciling paradigms that name no strategic choice actors make and test no strategic outcome.
  - *why*: A conceptual typology of strategies entrepreneurs use with audiences was SMJ, so conceptual work alone should not be penalized.
- **ADDED**: The dependent variable is internal organizational form, such as role structure or incidence of organizational change, with no performance or competitive consequence.
  - *why*: Non-SMJ counterparts studied functional-role development or change incidence, while SMJ winners studied performance or strategic action.
