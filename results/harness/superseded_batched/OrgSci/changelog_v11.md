# Changelog v11 (revision of v9)

**REJECTED** at iteration 11: val accuracy 90.0% (vs SMJ 100.0%, vs MgmtSci 78.9%); best so far 91.2%, threshold 90.2%. Revision of v9.

Train pool: v9 judged 39 train pairs, accuracy 94.9% (vs SMJ 100.0%, vs MgmtSci 90.0%). Reflection saw 2 misjudged, 7 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Advances core organization-theory conversations: organizational learning and search, legitimacy, optimal distinctiveness, categories, status dynamics, entrepreneurial creation, inequality inside organizations.
  - *now*: Advances core organization-theory conversations: learning, search and exploration (e.g., overcoming local search via hires or networks), legitimacy, categories, status, entrepreneurship, intra-organizational inequality.
  - *why*: Firm-level exploration via hiring as a conduit for distant knowledge was only weakly favored over individual-productivity shock studies.
- **EDITED**: Combines economic levers (ownership, incentives, pay, formal controls) with relational or behavioral lenses, showing when formal practices enable or crowd out social mechanisms.
  - *now*: Combines economic levers (incentives, monitoring, pay, formal controls) with relational or values-based lenses, showing when formal controls enable, crowd out or negate social mechanisms.
  - *why*: An agency-plus-relational study showing monitoring negates values communication was misjudged; the rule needed to name monitoring and values explicitly.
- **EDITED**: Accepts formal or simulation models, including agency models, when driven by actors' divergent beliefs, learning or policies that produce unintended consequences.
  - *now*: Accepts formal or simulation models of boundedly rational actors—fallible judgment, learning, search, aggregating views—where organizational structures or policies yield unintended consequences.
  - *why*: The board rewarded a stylized principal-agent optimization model; Organization Science's models feature bounded rationality, judgment aggregation and unintended consequences.
- **EDITED**: Welcomes inductive qualitative studies building new constructs or process models of identity tensions, discourse, inequality or unintended consequences of organizational practices.
  - *now*: Welcomes inductive qualitative or purely conceptual work building new constructs, process models, or organization-level constructs (e.g., climates, collective resources) with antecedents and consequences.
  - *why*: Merged two closely related method rules to free a slot for experimental work without losing the conceptual-paper anchor.
- **DROPPED**: Welcomes purely conceptual papers developing new organization-level constructs, such as climates or collective relational resources, with their antecedents and consequences.
  - *why*: Folded into the merged qualitative/conceptual rule.
- **ADDED**: Uses field or lab experiments to test how a managerial practice or organizational lever shapes coordination, collaboration or conduct, revealing theorized trade-offs.
  - *why*: Experiments that test managerial practices were undervalued compared with formal-economic or archival rivals.
- **EDITED**: Formal models built on financing, capital-structure, contracting or market frictions tied to strategy or profit payoffs, rather than actors' beliefs, learning or adaptation.
  - *now*: Contract-economics or mechanism-design logic—self-enforcing agreements, discounted relationship value, financing, optimal principal-agent processes solved for optimum—rather than actors' beliefs, learning or social relations.
  - *why*: Relational-contract economics and optimal-process principal-agent models, including in empirical papers, mark the non-Organization Science paper.
- **EDITED**: Explanation hinges on cross-national cultural values, legal origins or a country's historical regime; local social categories illustrating general mechanisms like homophily are fine.
  - *now*: Explanation hinges on national legal origins or a country's historical regime; regional social context (e.g., generalized trust) or local categories illustrating general mechanisms are fine.
  - *why*: The rule wrongly penalized a study using regional generalized trust as a sociological context variable.
- **EDITED**: Explains careers, pay, productivity or gaps via individual skills, credentials or labor-market screening, without the organization's relationships, status, structure, power or process.
  - *now*: Explains careers, pay, productivity or gaps via individual skills, skill development, credentials or labor-market screening, without the organization's relationships, status, structure, power or process.
  - *why*: Career-mobility studies explained by skill development, not relationships, belonged elsewhere but were only weakly flagged.
- **EDITED**: Headlines a precise effect size, or merely the existence of a bias, penalty, backfire or gap, as the core result rather than a theorized organizational process.
  - *now*: Headlines precise effect sizes or percentage gaps, or merely the existence of a bias, penalty, backfire or gap, rather than a theorized organizational process.
  - *why*: Abstracts that lead with percentage-point gaps and effect magnitudes repeatedly belonged to the other journal.
