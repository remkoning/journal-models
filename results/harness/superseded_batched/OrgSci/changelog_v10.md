# Changelog v10 (revision of v9)

**REJECTED** at iteration 10: val accuracy 83.8% (vs SMJ 100.0%, vs MgmtSci 65.8%); best so far 91.2%, threshold 90.2%. Revision of v9.

Train pool: v9 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 85.0% (vs SMJ 82.5%, vs MgmtSci 87.5%). Reflection saw 8 misjudged, 1 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Posits countervailing or self-limiting effects: the same feature that yields an advantage at one stage or condition creates a disadvantage at another.
  - *now*: Posits countervailing or self-limiting effects, where an advantage in one stage or condition becomes a liability in another, and shows how organizations navigate that tension.
  - *why*: OS winners often show how firms work around a paradox their own claims create, not only how audiences penalize them.
- **EDITED**: Advances core organization-theory conversations: organizational learning and search, legitimacy, optimal distinctiveness, categories, status dynamics, entrepreneurial creation, inequality inside organizations.
  - *now*: Advances core organization-theory conversations: organizational learning, search and its cognitive microfoundations, legitimacy, optimal distinctiveness, categories, status dynamics, entrepreneurship, intra-organizational inequality.
  - *why*: A lab study of the cognitive antecedents of search was published in OS but got penalized under the 'microfoundations' red flag.
- **EDITED**: Reconciles conflicting findings, competing camps or competing views through contingencies built from named theoretical mechanisms, not convenient proxies or practical boundary conditions.
  - *now*: Challenges a dominant explanation of an established regularity with an alternative mechanism, or reconciles competing camps through contingencies built from named theoretical mechanisms.
  - *why*: OS published a paper offering an alternative explanation to the behavioral-theory account of a known empirical paradox; the board missed it.
- **EDITED**: Accepts formal or simulation models, including agency models, when driven by actors' divergent beliefs, learning or policies that produce unintended consequences.
  - *now*: Accepts formal or simulation models of organizational learning, search or adaptation in which actors' divergent beliefs or policies produce emergent, unintended consequences.
  - *why*: A formal model of an executive bias predicting firm risk drew wrong credit under the old wording; it was not an OS paper.
- **EDITED**: Welcomes inductive qualitative studies building new constructs or process models of identity tensions, discourse, inequality or unintended consequences of organizational practices.
  - *now*: Welcomes inductive qualitative or mixed-method studies building new constructs or process models of identity, status, discourse, inequality or unintended consequences of practices.
  - *why*: OS published a study that moved from qualitative work to experiments to theorize status shifts; the old rule was too narrow to give it credit.
- **EDITED**: Welcomes purely conceptual papers developing new organization-level constructs, such as climates or collective relational resources, with their antecedents and consequences.
  - *now*: Welcomes conceptual papers developing new organization-level constructs, and dissertation summaries integrating several essays and theories around one organizational phenomenon.
  - *why*: A multi-essay dissertation summary published in OS was underrated; this rule now covers that format.
- **EDITED**: Theorizes temporal or reciprocal dynamics, e.g., how status, evaluation and performance feed back, reinforce or decouple over repeated rounds.
  - *now*: Theorizes temporal or reciprocal dynamics, e.g., how status, evaluation, performance or people's movement between organizations feed back, reinforce, decay or decouple over time.
  - *why*: OS winner theorized how advantages from people moving between organizations depend on timing and fade.
- **EDITED**: Theorizes how internal organizational design—delegation, autonomy, internal resource or capital allocation, parent–unit linkages, interorganizational relationships—shapes units' behavior, often across multiple levels.
  - *now*: Theorizes internal design and firm boundaries—delegation, capital allocation, make-or-buy, theory-of-the-firm debates, interorganizational arrangements—shaping unit behavior or industry structure.
  - *why*: OS papers on contractual vs. capability boundaries and on collaborative arrangements reshaping vertical structure were judged weakly.
- **EDITED**: Formal models built on financing, capital-structure, contracting or market frictions tied to strategy or profit payoffs, rather than actors' beliefs, learning or adaptation.
  - *now*: Formal models built on financing, capital-structure, contracting, real options or market frictions tied to strategy or profit payoffs, or on executive biases predicting firm risk.
  - *why*: Two non-OS papers were not flagged: an executive-bias model tested on risk taking, and a growth-option valuation paper.
- **EDITED**: The main contribution is a new measure, index, dataset, typology, literature review or research-agenda editorial, rather than a theoretical mechanism.
  - *now*: The main contribution is a new measure, index, dataset, typology, literature review or research agenda; this is a stronger warning than an effect-size headline.
  - *why*: A paper centered on a new index tied 50/50 with an OS paper that reported an effect size; the measure flag needs more weight.
- **EDITED**: Strategy framing: competitive advantage, firm performance or stock-market returns of strategic moves (acquisitions, brokerage positions), capability microfoundations, strategic human capital, with thin organizational mechanism.
  - *now*: Strategy framing: competitive advantage, firm performance, growth-option value or investor reactions and abnormal returns to strategic or political moves, with thin organizational mechanism.
  - *why*: An investor-reaction event study tied with an OS paper; 'capability microfoundations' wrongly counted against an OS search study.
- **EDITED**: Explanation hinges on cross-national cultural values, legal origins or a country's historical regime; local social categories illustrating general mechanisms like homophily are fine.
  - *now*: Explanation hinges on cross-national cultural values, legal origins or a country's historical regime; national contexts explained through status, identity or homophily mechanisms are fine.
  - *why*: An OS paper set in emerging economies that explained outcomes through status shifts was likely penalized for its national setting.
- **EDITED**: Headlines a precise effect size, or merely the existence of a bias, penalty, backfire or gap, as the core result rather than a theorized organizational process.
  - *now*: Headlines precise percentages or merely the existence of a bias, penalty or gap as the core result, rather than a theorized organizational or status process.
  - *why*: A non-OS hiring-bias paper that led with percentage effects tied with an OS paper built on a status mechanism.
