# Changelog v9 (revision of v7)

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 9: val accuracy 91.2% (vs SMJ 100.0%, vs MgmtSci 81.6%); best so far 91.2%, threshold 90.2%. Revision of v7.

Train pool: v7 judged 39 train pairs, accuracy 87.2% (vs SMJ 95.0%, vs MgmtSci 78.9%). Reflection saw 5 misjudged, 4 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Theorizes how audiences read signals, claims, categories or actor attributes, and how organizations trigger, avoid or resolve the resulting evaluation penalties.
  - *now*: Theorizes how audiences read signals, claims, language, categories or actor attributes, and how sender characteristics trigger, avoid or resolve evaluation penalties.
  - *why*: An audience-reading-language paper whose effect depends on sender attributes lost to a paper about advice and learning; the rule needed to cover language and sender contingencies explicitly.
- **EDITED**: Posits countervailing effects or trade-offs across stages, life cycles or conditions, e.g., a practice or technology shifting costs from one stage to another.
  - *now*: Posits countervailing or self-limiting effects: the same feature that yields an advantage at one stage or condition creates a disadvantage at another.
  - *why*: A paper where an advantage at generation turns into a disadvantage at selection lost to a descriptive behavioral-comparison experiment.
- **EDITED**: Uses experiments or multimethod designs pairing economic (agency, incentive) and behavioral or relational lenses, e.g., showing formal controls crowd out value-based or social levers.
  - *now*: Combines economic levers (ownership, incentives, pay, formal controls) with relational or behavioral lenses, showing when formal practices enable or crowd out social mechanisms.
  - *why*: An archival paper where an ownership practice enables social-interdependence benefits was missed because the rule required experimental designs.
- **EDITED**: Accepts formal or simulation models deriving how actors' beliefs, learning or organizational policies produce unintended consequences, such as equity practices backfiring.
  - *now*: Accepts formal or simulation models, including agency models, when driven by actors' divergent beliefs, learning or policies that produce unintended consequences.
  - *why*: An agency model driven by principal–agent belief misalignment was rejected; belief-driven agency models do fit the journal.
- **EDITED**: Quasi-experimental identification (instruments, exogenous shocks, unexpected deaths, quasi-random assignment) foregrounded, or closing with efficiency or policy prescriptions; theory-testing lab or field experiments are not flagged.
  - *now*: Policy-evaluation framing: one law, shock or program's causal effect identified and closed with prescriptions, without contrasting theorized mechanisms; mechanism-testing quasi-experiments are fine.
  - *why*: A difference-in-differences paper testing representation versus symbolic-compliance mechanisms was wrongly penalized relative to a single-policy evaluation.
- **EDITED**: Game-theoretic or optimal-design models of strategic interaction, incentives or contracting; Organization Science formal models instead derive learning, belief or adaptation dynamics.
  - *now*: Formal models built on financing, capital-structure, contracting or market frictions tied to strategy or profit payoffs, rather than actors' beliefs, learning or adaptation.
  - *why*: The flag hit a belief-misalignment agency model that was in Organization Science, while finance-friction models paired with strategy outcomes belong elsewhere.
- **EDITED**: Explanation hinges on cross-national culture, legal origins, or one country's distinctive historical regime or campaign, rather than a general organizational mechanism.
  - *now*: Explanation hinges on cross-national cultural values, legal origins or a country's historical regime; local social categories illustrating general mechanisms like homophily are fine.
  - *why*: A homophily paper using one country's social categories was risky under this flag; it should not be penalized like cultural-values explanations.
- **EDITED**: Quantitative pattern-finding whose mechanisms are guessed after the fact; this does not apply to inductive qualitative theory-building.
  - *now*: Quantitative pattern-finding with no mechanism theorized beforehand; post hoc checks of mechanisms that were hypothesized up front are fine.
  - *why*: A paper with a priori hypotheses that also ran post hoc mechanism analyses was wrongly treated as guessing mechanisms after the fact.
- **EDITED**: Headlines a precise effect size, or merely the existence of a bias, penalty or gap, as the core result rather than a theorized organizational process.
  - *now*: Headlines a precise effect size, or merely the existence of a bias, penalty, backfire or gap, as the core result rather than a theorized organizational process.
  - *why*: A policy-backfire finding with an assumed reaction mechanism attracted the board as an 'unintended consequence' when it is really just evidence that an effect exists.
