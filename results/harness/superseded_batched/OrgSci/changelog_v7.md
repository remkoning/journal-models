# Changelog v7 (revision of v4)

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 7: val accuracy 91.2% (vs SMJ 97.6%, vs MgmtSci 84.2%); best so far 88.8%, threshold 87.8%. Revision of v4.

Train pool: v4 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 95.0% (vs SMJ 95.0%, vs MgmtSci 95.0%). Reflection saw 2 misjudged, 7 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Uses experiments, multimethod designs (e.g., qualitative case plus experiment) or field data to isolate mechanisms, including how formal controls and social levers interact.
  - *now*: Uses experiments or multimethod designs pairing economic (agency, incentive) and behavioral or relational lenses, e.g., showing formal controls crowd out value-based or social levers.
  - *why*: The board missed an experiment combining agency and relational lenses on controls versus values; this rule needed to state that pattern sharply enough to outweigh its 'causal evidence' wording.
- **EDITED**: Links individual beliefs, cognition or relationships to network structure, hierarchy or group processes, showing how position and lay beliefs about the social order shape behavior.
  - *now*: Links individual beliefs, cognition or relationships to network structure, formal or informal hierarchy, or group processes such as integration, helping and mobility.
  - *why*: Organization Science papers on mobility and homophily stress relationships and hierarchy; their counterparts rely on skills or consumer bias. Low-confidence wins needed this stated more firmly.
- **ADDED**: Theorizes how internal organizational design—delegation, autonomy, internal resource or capital allocation, parent–unit linkages, interorganizational relationships—shapes units' behavior, often across multiple levels.
  - *why*: Organization Science papers on internal capital markets and research autonomy lost to network or middle-manager counterparts, so internal design mechanisms needed explicit credit.
- **EDITED**: Economics-style causal identification (instruments, exogenous legal or policy shocks) foregrounded, or program and law evaluation closing with efficiency or policy prescriptions.
  - *now*: Quasi-experimental identification (instruments, exogenous shocks, unexpected deaths, quasi-random assignment) foregrounded, or closing with efficiency or policy prescriptions; theory-testing lab or field experiments are not flagged.
  - *why*: The flag wrongly counted against a theory-testing experiment, while counterparts built on unexpected deaths or quasi-random assignment belonged elsewhere.
- **EDITED**: Framed for strategy audiences: competitive advantage, capability microfoundations, strategic human capital, scale or scope advantages, with thin sociological or behavioral mechanism.
  - *now*: Strategy framing: competitive advantage, firm performance or stock-market returns of strategic moves (acquisitions, brokerage positions), capability microfoundations, strategic human capital, with thin organizational mechanism.
  - *why*: Counterparts that judged brokerage or acquisitions by firm performance or announcement returns were wrongly preferred or barely rejected.
- **EDITED**: Explains pay, productivity or wage gaps via individual skills, credentials or labor-market screening, without the employing organization's status, structure, power or process.
  - *now*: Explains careers, pay, productivity or gaps via individual skills, credentials or labor-market screening, without the organization's relationships, status, structure, power or process.
  - *why*: A career-mobility counterpart explained by skill development lost to a relational-integration paper; the flag now covers careers and relationships.
