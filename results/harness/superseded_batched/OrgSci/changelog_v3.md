Train pool: v2 judged 39 train pairs, accuracy 92.3% (vs SMJ 100.0%, vs MgmtSci 85.0%). Reflection saw 4 misjudged, 5 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

  - *now*: Links individual cognition, relationships or network ties to formal and informal hierarchy, group processes or social integration, showing how position conditions outcomes.
  - *why*: OS picks favored network/homophily and relational integration studies over consumer-response or skill-based career studies.

  - *now*: Advances core organization-theory conversations: organizational learning and search, legitimacy, optimal distinctiveness, categories, status dynamics, entrepreneurial creation, inequality inside organizations.

  - *now*: Frames the contribution as reconciling conflicting prior findings, competing camps or competing mechanisms through contingencies, not just estimating an effect.
  - *why*: OS article reconciled two literature camps via contingencies; counterpart argued policy optimality.

  - *now*: Uses experiments or field data to separate mechanisms, or to show how formal controls and normative or social levers interact, e.g., one crowding out another.
  - *why*: OS published an experiment showing monitoring negates values communication, blending agency and relational views; board underrated it.

  - *now*: Accepts studies of sourcing, firm boundaries or interorganizational arrangements when social context such as generalized trust, norms or relationships drives the choice.
