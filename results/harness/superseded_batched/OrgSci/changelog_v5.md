# Changelog v5 (revision of v4)

**REJECTED** at iteration 5: val accuracy 83.8% (vs SMJ 97.6%, vs MgmtSci 68.4%); best so far 88.8%, threshold 87.8%. Revision of v4.

Train pool: v4 judged 39 train pairs, accuracy 89.7% (vs SMJ 90.0%, vs MgmtSci 89.5%). Reflection saw 5 misjudged, 4 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Theorizes how audiences read signals, claims, categories or actor attributes, and how organizations trigger, avoid or resolve the resulting evaluation penalties.
  - *now*: Theorizes how actors interpret signals, feedback, claims or categories: audiences evaluating organizations, or organizations reading market feedback to choose positions.
  - *why*: The board over-favored abstracts about audiences penalizing firms. It missed an Organization Science paper on organizations reading market feedback to choose their positions.
- **EDITED**: Explains with sociological or behavioral constructs such as status, legitimacy, identity, authenticity, trust, reciprocity, reputation or symbolic compliance, not mainly economic efficiency.
  - *now*: Explains with sociological or behavioral constructs such as status, legitimacy, identity, authenticity, trust, attention, symbolic compliance or stakeholder salience, not mainly economic efficiency.
  - *why*: Organization Science quasi-experiments won on attention and stakeholder-salience mechanisms, which the old list did not name.
- **EDITED**: Reconciles conflicting findings, competing camps or competing views through contingencies built from named theoretical mechanisms, not convenient proxies or practical boundary conditions.
  - *now*: Reconciles conflicting findings, puzzles or competing camps through contingencies built from named theoretical mechanisms, not convenient proxies or practical boundary conditions.
  - *why*: Papers that resolve an established empirical puzzle also signal Organization Science.
- **EDITED**: Uses experiments, multimethod designs (e.g., qualitative case plus experiment) or field data to isolate mechanisms, including how formal controls and social levers interact.
  - *now*: Uses experiments, multimethod designs or field data to isolate mechanisms, including how formal controls and social levers interact.
  - *why*: Shortened to make room; meaning unchanged.
- **EDITED**: Accepts formal or simulation models deriving how actors' beliefs, learning or organizational policies produce unintended consequences, such as equity practices backfiring.
  - *now*: Accepts formal, simulation or agency models when they derive how actors' divergent beliefs, learning, search or organizational structure produce outcomes or unintended consequences.
  - *why*: Organization Science published a closed-form incentive model built on board–CEO belief misalignment, which the board wrongly rejected.
- **ADDED**: Theorizes organization design: structure, departmentalization, division of labor, coordination and communication costs, and how structures fit or adapt to changing tasks.
  - *why*: An organization-structure model lost to an individual decision-cognition paper; the rulebook had no organization-design rule.
- **EDITED**: Welcomes inductive qualitative studies building new constructs or process models of identity tensions, discourse, inequality or unintended consequences of organizational practices.
  - *now*: Welcomes inductive qualitative or purely conceptual work building new constructs or process models, e.g., identity tensions, climates, unintended consequences of practices.
  - *why*: Merged with the conceptual-paper rule to make room for organization design.
- **DROPPED**: Welcomes purely conceptual papers developing new organization-level constructs, such as climates or collective relational resources, with their antecedents and consequences.
  - *why*: Merged into the qualitative/conceptual theory-building rule.
- **EDITED**: Economics-style causal identification (instruments, exogenous legal or policy shocks) foregrounded, or program and law evaluation closing with efficiency or policy prescriptions.
  - *now*: Program or law evaluation headlining the policy's effect and a policy verdict, with economic or post-hoc mechanisms; quasi-experiments testing named social mechanisms are fine.
  - *why*: Organization Science published difference-in-differences papers theorizing symbolic compliance or stakeholder salience. Pure policy-backfire and economic cost-benefit evaluations went elsewhere.
- **EDITED**: Game-theoretic or optimal-design models of strategic interaction, incentives or contracting; Organization Science formal models instead derive learning, belief or adaptation dynamics.
  - *now*: Industrial-organization models of market competition, licensing, pricing or equilibrium tested on industry panels, rather than models of organizational actors' beliefs, learning or structure.
  - *why*: A blanket ban on incentive models wrongly rejected an Organization Science agency model. Industry-competition models still go elsewhere.
- **EDITED**: Framed for strategy audiences: competitive advantage, capability microfoundations, strategic human capital, scale or scope advantages, with thin sociological or behavioral mechanism.
  - *now*: Framed for strategy audiences: competitive advantage, capability microfoundations, internal capital allocation, diversification, executive decision cognition, with thin social or organizational mechanism.
  - *why*: Corporate capital-allocation and strategic decision-maker cognition papers went to strategy journals, not Organization Science.
- **EDITED**: Headlines a precise effect size, or merely the existence of a bias, penalty or gap, as the core result rather than a theorized organizational process.
  - *now*: Headlines a precise effect size, or merely the existence of a bias, penalty or backfire, as the core result rather than a theorized organizational process.
  - *why*: Simply showing that a reform backfired, with the mechanism guessed afterward, marked the non-Organization Science paper.
