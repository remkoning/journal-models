# Organization Science rulebook v3

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 3: val accuracy 87.5% (vs SMJ 97.6%, vs MgmtSci 76.3%); best so far 86.2%, threshold 85.2%. Revision of v2.

## What Organization Science values
1. Theorizes how audiences read signals, claims, categories or actor attributes, and how those evaluations constrain or enable what organizations can do.
2. Posits countervailing effects, paradoxes or trade-offs, such as a benefit at one stage or condition and a penalty at another, and specifies contingencies.
3. Explains with sociological or behavioral constructs such as status, legitimacy, identity, authenticity, trust, reciprocity, reputation or symbolic compliance, not mainly economic efficiency.
4. Links individual cognition, relationships or network ties to formal and informal hierarchy, group processes or social integration, showing how position conditions outcomes.
5. Advances core organization-theory conversations: organizational learning and search, legitimacy, optimal distinctiveness, categories, status dynamics, entrepreneurial creation, inequality inside organizations.
6. Frames the contribution as reconciling conflicting prior findings, competing camps or competing mechanisms through contingencies, not just estimating an effect.
7. Uses experiments or field data to separate mechanisms, or to show how formal controls and normative or social levers interact, e.g., one crowding out another.
8. Accepts formal or simulation models deriving how actors' beliefs, learning or organizational policies produce unintended consequences, such as equity practices backfiring.
9. Builds moderators from named theoretical mechanisms about actors, audiences or organizations, rather than from convenient proxies or practical boundary conditions.
10. Welcomes inductive qualitative studies that build new constructs showing how organizational practices produce identity tensions, inequality or unintended consequences.
11. Theorizes temporal or reciprocal dynamics, e.g., how status, evaluation and performance feed back, reinforce or decouple over repeated rounds.
12. Accepts studies of sourcing, firm boundaries or interorganizational arrangements when social context such as generalized trust, norms or relationships drives the choice.

## What Organization Science does not value / red flags
1. Economics-style evaluation of a public program, law or policy using exogenous variation, closing with efficiency or policy prescriptions instead of organizational theory.
2. Formal incentive, contracting or optimal-design models (wages, effort, self-enforcing agreements, government procurement bundling) centered on efficiency suggest economics, finance or strategy journals.
3. The main contribution is a new measure, index, dataset or typology, even when pitched as enabling future theory, rather than a theoretical mechanism.
4. Framed for strategy audiences: competitive advantage, capability microfoundations, strategic human capital, scale or scope advantages, with thin sociological or behavioral mechanism.
5. Explanation hinges on cross-national culture, legal origins, or one country's distinctive regime or campaign, rather than a general organizational mechanism such as legitimacy.
6. Quantitative pattern-finding whose mechanisms are guessed after the fact; this does not apply to inductive qualitative theory-building.
7. Explains individual productivity, pay growth or promotion through skill or human-capital accumulation, without relational, cognitive or organizational process.
8. Headlines precise percentage-point effect sizes or design prescriptions as the core result, in any setting, rather than a theorized social mechanism.
