# Changelog v4 (revision of v3)

**ACCEPTED** at iteration 4: val accuracy 88.8% (vs SMJ 97.6%, vs MgmtSci 78.9%); best so far 87.5%, threshold 86.5%. Revision of v3.

Train pool: v3 judged 40 train pairs, accuracy 86.2% (vs SMJ 100.0%, vs MgmtSci 72.5%). Reflection saw 6 misjudged, 3 low-confidence and 3 anchor pairs.

- **EDITED**: Theorizes how audiences read signals, claims, categories or actor attributes, and how those evaluations constrain or enable what organizations can do.
  - *now*: Theorizes how audiences read signals, claims, categories or actor attributes, and how organizations trigger, avoid or resolve the resulting evaluation penalties.
  - *why*: The Organization Science papers explain how firms can resolve audience penalties, not only show that the penalties exist.
- **EDITED**: Posits countervailing effects, paradoxes or trade-offs, such as a benefit at one stage or condition and a penalty at another, and specifies contingencies.
  - *now*: Posits countervailing effects or trade-offs across stages, life cycles or conditions, e.g., a practice or technology shifting costs from one stage to another.
  - *why*: Winners theorized effects that change across venture life stages or move costs across process stages; losers documented a single effect.
- **EDITED**: Links individual cognition, relationships or network ties to formal and informal hierarchy, group processes or social integration, showing how position conditions outcomes.
  - *now*: Links individual beliefs, cognition or relationships to network structure, hierarchy or group processes, showing how position and lay beliefs about the social order shape behavior.
  - *why*: The Organization Science status paper tied lay beliefs to network behavior, while the other one studied the psychology of individual effort.
- **EDITED**: Frames the contribution as reconciling conflicting prior findings, competing camps or competing mechanisms through contingencies, not just estimating an effect.
  - *now*: Reconciles conflicting findings, competing camps or competing views through contingencies built from named theoretical mechanisms, not convenient proxies or practical boundary conditions.
  - *why*: Merged with the moderator rule to free a slot; competing-views framing reliably marked Organization Science papers.
- **DROPPED**: Builds moderators from named theoretical mechanisms about actors, audiences or organizations, rather than from convenient proxies or practical boundary conditions.
  - *why*: Folded into the reconciling-contingencies rule.
- **EDITED**: Uses experiments or field data to separate mechanisms, or to show how formal controls and normative or social levers interact, e.g., one crowding out another.
  - *now*: Uses experiments, multimethod designs (e.g., qualitative case plus experiment) or field data to isolate mechanisms, including how formal controls and social levers interact.
  - *why*: An Organization Science paper that combined a qualitative case with experiments lost to a large panel study of firms.
- **EDITED**: Welcomes inductive qualitative studies that build new constructs showing how organizational practices produce identity tensions, inequality or unintended consequences.
  - *now*: Welcomes inductive qualitative studies building new constructs or process models of identity tensions, discourse, inequality or unintended consequences of organizational practices.
  - *why*: Discursive process models from qualitative studies are Organization Science work; widened the scope to cover them.
- **ADDED**: Welcomes purely conceptual papers developing new organization-level constructs, such as climates or collective relational resources, with their antecedents and consequences.
  - *why*: A conceptual theory paper building an organization-level construct lost to a one-company empirical study.
- **EDITED**: Economics-style evaluation of a public program, law or policy using exogenous variation, closing with efficiency or policy prescriptions instead of organizational theory.
  - *now*: Economics-style causal identification (instruments, exogenous legal or policy shocks) foregrounded, or program and law evaluation closing with efficiency or policy prescriptions.
  - *why*: Papers that foreground instrumental-variable identification appeared elsewhere, even when they used network language.
- **EDITED**: Formal incentive, contracting or optimal-design models (wages, effort, self-enforcing agreements, government procurement bundling) centered on efficiency suggest economics, finance or strategy journals.
  - *now*: Game-theoretic or optimal-design models of strategic interaction, incentives or contracting; Organization Science formal models instead derive learning, belief or adaptation dynamics.
  - *why*: Game-theoretic models of firm–activist and firm–subsidiary strategy appeared elsewhere even without an efficiency focus.
- **EDITED**: The main contribution is a new measure, index, dataset or typology, even when pitched as enabling future theory, rather than a theoretical mechanism.
  - *now*: The main contribution is a new measure, index, dataset, typology, literature review or research-agenda editorial, rather than a theoretical mechanism.
  - *why*: A special-issue introduction proposing a research agenda nearly beat an empirical Organization Science study.
- **EDITED**: Explanation hinges on cross-national culture, legal origins, or one country's distinctive regime or campaign, rather than a general organizational mechanism such as legitimacy.
  - *now*: Explanation hinges on cross-national culture, legal origins, or one country's distinctive historical regime or campaign, rather than a general organizational mechanism.
  - *why*: A historical study set in one country's distinctive regime was preferred wrongly; made the historical case explicit.
- **EDITED**: Explains individual productivity, pay growth or promotion through skill or human-capital accumulation, without relational, cognitive or organizational process.
  - *now*: Explains pay, productivity or wage gaps via individual skills, credentials or labor-market screening, without the employing organization's status, structure, power or process.
  - *why*: The old rule penalized human-capital papers that are moderated by organizational structure or status, and missed labor-market screening explanations.
- **EDITED**: Headlines precise percentage-point effect sizes or design prescriptions as the core result, in any setting, rather than a theorized social mechanism.
  - *now*: Headlines a precise effect size, or merely the existence of a bias, penalty or gap, as the core result rather than a theorized organizational process.
  - *why*: Papers that document a bias or an exit effect lost; Organization Science winners also report effect sizes, but only after theorizing the process.
